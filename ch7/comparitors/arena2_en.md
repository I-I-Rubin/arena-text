### Third Chapter. (gemini 4 argon high)

## The Production of Absolute Surplus-Value.

## 1\) *Labour-Process and Valorization-Process (*Verwerthungsprozess*)*

The use of labour-power is *labour itself*. The buyer of labour-power consumes it by having its seller *labour (*arbeiten*)*. The latter becomes hereby *actu* self-activating labour-power, *worker*, what he earlier was only *potentia*. In order to present his labour in *commodities*, he must above all present it in *use values*, *things(*Sachen*)* that serve for the meeting of needs of some type or other. It is thus a particular use value, a determinate *article*, that the capitalist has made by the worker. The *production of use values*, or *goods*, does not alter its *universal* nature by the fact that it proceeds *for* the capitalist and under his control. The *labour-process* is therefore first of all to be considered in its *abstract* moments, independently of every *determinate social form*.

The labour-process is first of all a process between man and nature, a process wherein he mediates, regulates and controls his metabolism (*Stoffwechsel*) with nature through his own deed. Man confronts the natural stuff (*Naturstoff*) itself as a power of nature. The natural forces belonging to his corporeality, arms and legs, head and hand, he sets in movement in order to assimilate the natural stuff to himself in a form usable for his own life. By acting through this movement upon nature outside him and altering it, he alters at the same time his own nature. He develops the potencies (*Potenzen*) slumbering in it and subjects the play of its forces to his own sway. We do not have to do here with the first animal-like instinctual forms of the labour-process. From the state wherein the worker steps forth on the commodity-market as seller of his own labour-power, the state wherein the human labour-process had not yet stripped off its first instinct-like form is removed into the primordial background. We presuppose the labour-process in a form wherein it belongs exclusively *to man*. A spider performs operations that resemble those of the weaver, and a bee puts to shame by the building of its wax cells many a human master-builder. What however from the outset distinguishes the worst master-builder from the best bee is that he has built the cell in his head before he builds it in wax. At the end of the labour-process a result comes out which at the beginning of the same was already on hand in the *representation (*Vorstellung*) of the worker*, thus already *ideally (*ideell*)*. Not that he merely *effects (*bewirkt*)* a form-alteration of the natural, he at the same time *actualizes (*verwirklicht*)* in the natural *his purpose*, which he *knows*, which determines the type and manner of his doing as a law and to which he must subordinate his will. And this subordination is no single (*vereinzelter*) act. Apart from the exertion of the organs that labour, the *purposive will*, which manifests (*äussert*) itself as *attention*, is required for the whole duration of the labour-process, and all the more so, the less the labour by its own content and its type and manner of execution carries the worker along with it, the less he therefore enjoys it as the play of his own bodily and mental (*geistigen*) forces.

The simple moments of the labour-process are *purposive activity* or *labour itself*, its *object* and its *means*.

*The earth* (under which economically *water* too is included), as it originally equips man with provisions, ready-made means of life[^1], is found on hand without his assistance as the *universal object* of human labour. All things which labour merely detaches from their immediate connection with the whole of the earth are objects of labour found on hand by nature. Thus the fish that is caught, separated from its element of life, water, the timber that is felled in the primeval forest, the ore that is broken loose from its vein. If on the other hand the object of labour has itself already been so to speak filtered *through earlier labour*, then we call it *raw material*. E.g. the already broken-loose ore that is now washed out. All raw material is object of labour, but not every object of labour is raw material. The object of labour is raw material only as soon as it has already undergone an alteration mediated through labour.

The *means of labour* is a thing or a complex of things that the worker shoves between himself and the object of labour and that serve him as *conductor* of his activity onto this object. He uses the mechanical, physical, chemical properties of things in order to let them act as means of power upon other things, *in accordance with his purpose*[^2]. The object of which the worker *immediately* takes possession --- apart from the seizing of ready-made means of life, fruits e.g., waarbij his own bodily organs alone serve as means of labour --- is not the object of labour, but the means of labour. Thus the natural itself becomes an *organ* of his activity, an organ that he adds to his own bodily organs, lengthening his natural stature (*Gestalt*), in spite of the Bible. As the earth is his original larder, it is his original arsenal of means of labour. It delivers to him e.g. the *stone*, wherewith he throws, rubs, presses, cuts etc. *The earth itself* is a means of labour, yet presupposes for its service as a means of labour in agriculture in turn a whole series of other means of labour and an already relatively high development of labour-power[^3]. As soon as the labour-process is at all developed to any extent, it requires already laboured-on (*bearbeiteter*) means of labour. In the oldest caves of man we find stone tools and stone weapons. Alongside laboured-on *stone* and *wood*, in the beginning of human history the *tamed*, thus itself already through labour altered, bred *animal* plays the main role as means of labour[^4].// 

The use and the creation of means of labour, although in germ already proper to certain types of animals, characterize the *specifically human labour-process* and *Franklin* therefore defines man as "*a toolmaking animal*", a tool-fabricating animal. The same importance that the structure of bone-relics has for the cognition of the organization of extinct animal genera, relics of *means of labour* have for the judgment of extinct economic formations of society. Not *what* is made, but *how*, with what means of labour it is made, distinguishes the economic epochs[^5]. The means of labour are not only the gauge of the development of human labour-power, but also the index of the social relationships (*Verhältnisse*) under which labour is carried on. Among the means of labour themselves, the *mechanical means of labour*, whose totality one can call the *bone- and muscle-system of production*, offer much more decisive characteristic marks of a social epoch of production than such means of labour as serve only as containers of the object of labour, and whose totality can quite universally be designated as the *vascular system of production*, such as e.g. pipes, barrels, baskets, jugs etc. Only in chemical fabrication do they play a significant role.

In a wider sense the labour-process counts among its *means*, apart from the things which mediate the action (*Wirkung*) of labour upon its object, and therefore in one way or another serve as conductors of activity, all *objective (*gegenständlichen*) conditions* that are required at all so that the whole process may proceed. They do not enter directly into the labour-process, but without them the labour-process cannot proceed at all or only imperfectly. The universal means of labour of this type is again the *earth itself*, for it gives the worker the *locus standi* and to his process *the space of action (*Wirkungsraum*)* ( *field of employment*). Means of labour of this type already mediated through labour are e.g. work-buildings, canals, roads etc.

The *labour-process* is thus a process wherein the activity of man effects (*bewirkt*) through the means of labour an from the outset purposed alteration in the object of labour. This process extinguishes itself in the *product*. Its product is a *use value*, a natural stuff (*Naturstoff*) assimilated to human needs through form-alteration. Through the process, labour has bound itself together with its object. Labour is objectified (*vergegenständlicht*) and the object is laboured up (*verarbeitet*). What on the side of the worker appeared in the form of unrest (*Unruhe*), now appears as a resting property, in the form of being (*Sein*), on the side of the product. He has spun and the product is a spun-stuff (*Gespinnst*).

If one considers the whole process from the standpoint of its result, *the product*, then both, *means of labour* and *object of labour*, appear as *means of production*[^6] and labour itself as *productive labour*[^7].

When a use value comes out of the labour-process as *product*, other use values, themselves products of earlier labour-processes, enter into the process *as means of production*. The same use value that is the product of one labour-process forms the means of production of another. Products are therefore not only result, but at the same time *condition* of the labour-process.

With the exception of *extractive industry*, which finds its object of labour on hand by nature, such as mining, hunting, fishing etc. (tillage only insofar as it in the first instance breaks open the virgin earth itself), all branches of industry treat an *object* that is *raw material*, i.e. object of labour already filtered through labour, itself already product of an earlier labour-process. Thus e.g. *seed* in agriculture. Animals and plants, which one is accustomed to consider as products of nature, are not only products perhaps of last year's labour, but, in their present forms, products of a transmutation (*Umwandlung*) continued through many generations, under human control, by means of human labour. As concerns the *means of labour* in particular, however, their vast majority shows to the most superficial glance the trace of past labour.

The raw material can form the main substance of a product, or enter *only* as *auxiliary stuff (*Hilfsstoff*)* into its formation. The auxiliary stuff is *consumed by the means of labour*, as coal by a steam-engine, oil by the wheel, hay by a draft-horse, or *added to the raw material* in order to effect (*bewirken*) a material (*stoffliche*) alteration therein, as chlorine to unbleached linen, carbon to iron, dye to wool, or it supports the *performance of the labour* itself, as e.g. stuffs (*Stoffe*) used for the lighting and heating of the work-premises. The distinction between main stuff and auxiliary stuff blurs in chemical fabrication proper, because none of the applied raw materials *appears* again as the substance of the product[^8].

Since every thing possesses many properties and is therefore capable of different useful applications, the same product can form the raw material of very different labour-processes. Grain e.g. is raw material for miller, starch-manufacturer, distiller, cattle-breeder etc. It becomes raw material of its own production as seed. Similarly coal comes forth as product from the mining industry and enters into it as means of production.

The same product may serve in the same labour-process as means of labour and raw material. In cattle-fattening e.g., where the cattle, the laboured-on raw material, is at the same time means of manure-preparation.

A product can exist in a form ready for consumption and nevertheless anew become the raw material of another product, as the grape becomes the raw material of wine. Or the labour-process discharges its product in forms wherein it can be used *only* as raw material of a subsequent labour-process. Raw material in this state is called *semi-fabricate* and would better be called *stage-fabricate (*Stufenfabrikat*)*, as e.g. cotton, thread, yarn etc. Although itself already product, the original raw material may have to run through a whole ladder of different labour-processes, wherein in ever altered shape (*Gestalt*) it functions ever anew as raw material up to the last labour-process, which repels it from itself as ready *means of life* or ready *means of labour*.

One sees: whether *a use value* appears as *raw material, means of labour* or *product* depends altogether on its *determinate function in the labour-process*, on the place that it occupies *in it*, and with the change of this place those determinations change.

Through their entry as *means of production* into new labour-processes, products therefore lose the character of product. They function only still as objective (*gegenständliche*) factors of living labour. The spinner relates (*bezieht*) himself to the spindle only as means wherewith, and to the flax only as object which, he spins. To be sure, one cannot spin without spinning-material and spindle. The being-on-hand of these products is therefore presupposed at the beginning of the actual spinning-process. *In this process itself*, however, it is just as indifferent that flax and spindle are *products of past labour*, as in the actual nutrition-process it is indifferent that bread is the *product* of the past labours of peasant, miller, baker etc. Conversely. If means of production assert their character in the labour-process as products of past labour, they do so through their defects. A knife that does not cut, yarn that constantly tears etc., remind one vividly of cutler A and yarn-dresser E. In the successful product the mediation of its use-properties through past labour is extinguished.

A machine that does not serve in the labour-process is useless. Besides, it falls prey to the destructive force of natural metabolism (*Stoffwechsel*). Iron rusts, wood rots. Yarn that is not woven up or knitted up is spoiled cotton. Living labour must seize these things, awaken them from the dead, transform them from merely possible into actual (*wirkliche*) and acting (*wirkende*) use values. Licked by the fire of labour, appropriated as bodies of the same, animated (*begeistet*) to their conceptual and vocational functions in the process, they are indeed also consumed, but purposefully, as formation-elements of new use values.

If products on hand are not only the *results*, but also the *conditions of existence* of the labour-process, on the other hand their throwing-into the labour-process, thus their contact with living labour, is *the sole means to preserve and to actualize these products of past labour as use values*. The labour-process results at all only in *use value* insofar as its product is capable of entering as *means of life* into individual consumption or as *means of production* into a new labour-process.

In the labour-process its material (*stofflichen*) elements, its object and its means, are used up *as use values*. Labour devours them. The labour-process is thus *consumption-process*. It distinguishes this *productive consumption* from *individual consumption* that the latter consumes the products as *means of life of the living individual*, the former consumes them as *means of life of labour*, of his self-activating labour-power. The product of individual consumption is therefore *the consumer himself*, the result of productive consumption a *product* distinct (*unterschiednes*) from the consumer.

Insofar as means of labour and object of labour are themselves already *products*, results of past labour-processes, the labour-process is a process wherein *products are consumed in order to create products* or wherein *products serve as means of production of products*. Yet just as the labour-process originally proceeds only between man and the earth on hand without his assistance, there still always serve in it such means of production as, on hand by nature, present no combination of natural stuff (*Naturstoff*) and human labour.

The *labour-process*, as we have presented it in its simple and *abstract* moments, is purposive activity for the production of use values, appropriation of the natural for human needs, universal condition of the metabolism (*Stoffwechsel*) between man and nature, eternal natural condition of human life and therefore independent of every form of this life, rather equally common to all its forms of society. We had therefore in our presentation no need to present the worker in relationship (*Verhältniss*) to other workers. Man and his labour on the one side, nature and its stuffs (*Stoffe*) on the other side, sufficed. As little as one tastes on the wheat who has grown it, so little does one see on this process under what conditions it proceeds, whether under the brutal whip of the slave-overseer or under the anxious eye of the capitalist, whether Cincinnatus performs it in the tilling of his few jugera, or the savage who fells a beast with a stone[^9].

----

Let us return to our *capitalist in spe*. We left him after he had bought on the commodity-market all factors necessary to a labour-process, the *objective (*gegenständlichen*) factors* in the *means of production, the subjective factor in the labour-power*. The determinate type of these means of production and of this labour-power is governed by the type of labour-process for which they are determined, whether boots, yarn etc. are to be made. Our capitalist thus sets about consuming the commodity bought by him, *the labour-power*, i.e. *having* the bearer of the labour-power, *the worker, consume* the means of production through his labour. The *universal* nature of the labour-process naturally does not alter itself by the fact that the worker performs it for the capitalist instead of for himself. But also the *determinate type and manner* in which boots are made or yarn is spun cannot at first alter itself through the intervention of the capitalist. He must at first take the labour-power as he finds it on hand on the market, thus also its labour as it sprang up in a period when there were as yet no capitalists. The *transformation of the mode of production* itself through the subordination of labour under capital can occur only later and is therefore to be considered only later.

The labour-process, as it proceeds as *consumption-process of labour-power by the capitalist*, shows only two peculiar phenomena.

The worker labours *under the control of the capitalist*, to whom his labour belongs. The capitalist watches out that the labour is properly performed and that the means of production are purposively applied, thus no raw material is wasted and the instrument of labour is spared, i.e. only so far destroyed as is inseparable from its use in the labour.

Secondly, however: *the product is the property of the capitalist*, not of the immediate producer, the worker. The capitalist pays e.g. the *daily value of the labour-power*. Its use, like that of every other commodity, e.g. of a horse that he has hired for a day, belongs to him thus for the day. To the buyer of the commodity belongs the use of the commodity, and the possessor of labour-power in fact gives only the use value *sold* by him by giving *his labour*. From the moment when he stepped into the workshop of the capitalist, the *use value* of his labour-power, thus its use, *the labour*, belonged to the capitalist. By the *purchase of labour-power* the capitalist has *incorporated (*einverleibt*)* labour itself as a living fermenting-stuff into the dead formation-elements of the product likewise possessed by him. From his standpoint the *labour-process* is only the *consumption* of the commodity labour-power bought by him, which he however can consume only by *setting to (*zusetzt*) it means of production*. The labour-process is a process between things that the capitalist has *bought*, *between things belonging to him*. The *product of this process* belongs to him therefore quite as much as the product of the fermentation-process in his wine-cellar[^10].

The product --- the property of the capitalist --- is a *use value*, yarn, boots etc. But although boots e.g. to a certain extent form the basis of social progress and our capitalist is a decided man of progress, he does not fabricate the boots for their own sake. *Use value* is altogether not the thing "*qu'on aime pour lui même*" in *commodity-production*. Use values are here produced at all only because and insofar as they are *material (*materielles*) substrate, bearers of exchange value*. And for our capitalist it is a matter of two things. *First*, he wants to produce a use value that has an exchange value, an article determined for sale, a *commodity*. And secondly, he wants to produce a *commodity* whose value is *higher* than the *sum of values of the commodities required for its production*, the means of production and the labour-power, for which he *advanced* his good money on the commodity-market. He wants to produce not only a *use value*, but a *commodity*, not only use value, but exchange value, and not only *value*, but also *surplus-value*.

In fact, since it is a matter here of *commodity-production*, we have hitherto manifestly considered only *one side of the process*. Just as the *commodity* itself is unity of *use value and exchange value*, its *production-process* must be *unity of labour-process and value-formation-process*.

Let us thus consider the *production-process* now also as *value-formation-process*.

We know that the value of every commodity is determined by the quantum of the *labour materialized* in its use value, by the *labour-time socially necessary* for its production. This figures (*gilt*) also for the product that resulted for our capitalist as result of the labour-process. First of all, therefore, *the labour objectified (*vergegenständlichte*) in this product* is to be calculated.

Let it be e.g. *yarn*.

For the production of the yarn there was first needed its *raw material*, e.g. 10 pounds of cotton. What the value of the cotton is need not first be investigated, for the capitalist has bought it on the market at its value, e.g. at 10 sh. In the *price* of the cotton the labour required for its production is already presented as universal social labour. We want further to assume that the *spindle-mass consumed* in the working up of the cotton, which is representative (*repräsentirt*) for us of all other expended means of labour, possesses a value of 2 sh. If a gold-mass of 12 sh. is the product of 24 labour-hours or two labour-days, then it follows first of all that in the yarn two labour-days are objectified (*vergegenständlicht*).

The circumstance that the cotton has altered its form and the consumed spindle-mass has wholly disappeared must not mislead. According to the universal law of value, e.g. 10 lbs. of yarn are an equivalent for 10 lbs. of cotton and ¼ spindle, if the value of 40 lbs. of yarn = the value of 40 lbs. of cotton + the value of a whole spindle, i.e. if *the same labour-time* is required in order to produce both sides of this equality (*Gleichung*). In this case *the same labour-time* presents itself the one time in the use value yarn, the other time in the use values cotton and spindle. Exchange value is thus indifferent toward whether it appears in yarn, spindle or cotton. That spindle and cotton, instead of lying quietly beside one another, enter in the spinning-process into a combination which alters the form of their use value, *transforms them into yarn*, affects their exchange value just as little as if they had been transposed through *simple exchange* against an equivalent of yarn.

The labour-time required for the production of the cotton is part of the labour-time required for the production of the yarn, whose raw material it forms, and is therefore contained in the yarn. So it stands too with the labour-time that is required for the production of the spindle-mass, without whose wear-and-tear or consumption the cotton cannot be spun up[^11].

So far therefore as the *value of the yarn*, the *labour-time* required for its production, comes into consideration, *the different particular labour-processes, separated* in time and space, which must be run through in order to produce the cotton, to produce the used-up spindle-mass, finally to make yarn out of cotton and spindle, can be considered as different successive phases *of one and the same labour-process*. All labour contained in the yarn is *past labour*. That the labour-time required for the production of its formation-elements was *passed earlier*, stands in the pluperfect, whereas the labour immediately applied to the concluding process, spinning, stands closer to the present, in the perfect, is a thoroughly indifferent circumstance. If a determinate mass of labour, e.g. of 30 labour-days, is needed for the building of a house, it alters nothing in the *total quantum* of the labour-time incorporated (*einverleibten*) into the house that the 30th labour-day entered into the production 29 days later than the first labour-day. And thus the labour-time contained in the material of labour and means of labour can be considered quite as if it had merely been expended in an earlier stage of the spinning-process than the quantum of labour expended last in the *form of spinning*.

The values of the means of production, of the cotton and of the spindle, expressed in the price of 12 sh., form therefore component parts of the *yarn-value*, or of the value of the *product*.

Only two conditions must be fulfilled. First, cotton and spindle must actually have served for the production of a *use value*. In our case *yarn* must have come to be out of them.

*Which* use value bears it is indifferent to exchange value, but a *use value* must bear it. Secondly, it is presupposed that only the labour-time necessary under the given *social conditions of production* has been applied. Were therefore only 1 pound of cotton needed in order to spin 1 pound of yarn, then only 1 pound of cotton may be consumed in the formation of 1 pound of yarn. Just so does it stand with the spindle. Had the capitalist the fantasy of having spinning done with golden instead of with iron spindles, there would nevertheless count (*zählte*) in the *yarn-value* only the *socially necessary labour-time*, i.e. the labour-time necessary for the production of the iron spindle-mass.

We now know the *value-part* which the means of production, cotton and spindle, form in the *yarn-value*. It is equal to 12 sh., or the materiature of two labour-days. It is a matter therefore now of the value-part which *the labour of the spinner* itself sets to (*zusetzt*) the cotton.

We have now to consider this labour from a quite other point of view than during the *labour-process*. There it was a matter of the purposive activity of transforming cotton into yarn. The more purposive the labour, the better the yarn, all other circumstances presupposed as remaining equal. The labour of the spinner was specifically different from other productive labours, and this difference manifested itself subjectively and objectively (*objektiv*), in the particular purpose of spinning, its particular mode of operation, the particular nature of its means of production, the specific use value of its product. Cotton and spindle serve as *means of life of spinning-labour*, but one cannot make rifled cannons with them. Insofar on the other hand as the labour of the spinner is *value-forming*, i.e. *source of exchange value*, it is thoroughly not different from the labour of the cannon-borer, or, what lies closer to us here, from the labours that are actualized in the means of production of the yarn, the labours of the cotton-planter and of the spindle-maker. Only because of this *identity* can cotton-planting, spindle-making and spinning form merely *quantitatively* different parts of the *same* total value, the *yarn-value*. It is no longer a matter here of the *quality*, the constitution and the content of the labour, but only still of its *quantity*. This is simply to be *counted*. We assume that spinning-labour is *simple* labour, social average labour. One will see later that the contrary assumption would alter nothing in the matter (*Sache*).

During the labour-process the labour constantly transposes itself out of the form of unrest (*Unruhe*) into that of being (*Sein*), out of the form of movement into that of objectivity (*Gegenständlichkeit*). At the end of an hour the spinning-movement is presented in a certain quantum of yarn, thus a determinate quantum of labour, *one labour-hour*, is *objectified (*vergegenständlicht*)* in the cotton. We say labour-hour, for the spinning-labour figures (*gilt*) here only insofar as it is expenditure of labour-power, not insofar as it is the specific labour of *spinning*.

It is now decisively important that during the duration of the process, i.e. of the transformation of cotton into yarn, only the *socially necessary labour-time* is consumed. If under *normal*, i.e. average social conditions of production, a pounds of cotton must be transformed during *one* labour-hour into b pounds of yarn, then only that labour-day figures (*gilt*) as a labour-day of 12 hours which transforms 12 × a pounds of cotton into 12 × b pounds of yarn. For only the socially necessary labour-time counts (*zählt*) as *value-forming*.

*Raw material* and *product* appear here in a quite other light than from the standpoint of the labour-process proper. The raw material figures (*gilt*) here only as *absorber of a determinate quantum of labour*. Through this absorption it transforms itself in fact into yarn, because *spinning-labour* was set to (*zugesetzt*) it. But the product, the yarn, is now only a gauge of the labour absorbed by the cotton. If in one hour 1⅔ lbs. of cotton is spun up or transformed into 1⅔ lbs. of yarn, then 10 lbs. of yarn indicate 6 absorbed labour-hours. Determinate and *empirically established quanta of product* now present nothing than determinate quanta of labour, determinate measures (*Maße*) of congealed labour-time. They are only still *materiature* of one hour, two hours, one day *of social labour*.

That the labour is precisely spinning-labour, its material cotton and its product yarn, becomes here just as indifferent as that the *object of labour* is itself already product, thus *raw material*. Were the worker, instead of in the spinning-mill, occupied in the coal-mine, the object of labour, the coal, would be on hand by nature. Nevertheless a determinate quantum of coal broken loose from the bed, e.g. a hundredweight, would present a determinate quantum of absorbed labour.

In the *sale of labour-power* it was assumed that its daily value = 3 sh., and in the latter 6 labour-hours are embodied (*verkörpert*), that this quantum of labour is thus required in order to produce the average sum of the daily means of life of the worker. If our spinner now transforms during one labour-hour 1⅔ lbs. of cotton into 1⅔ lbs. of yarn[^12], then in 6 hours 10 lbs. of cotton into 10 lbs. of yarn. During the duration of the spinning-process the cotton thus absorbs 6 labour-hours. The same labour-time presents itself in a quantum of gold of 3 sh. Through the spinning itself, therefore, a value of 3 sh. was set to (*zugesetzt*) the cotton.

Let us look now at the *total value of the product*, of the 10 lbs. of yarn. In them 2½ labour-days are objectified (*vergegenständlicht*), 2 days contained in cotton and spindle-mass, ½ day of labour absorbed during the spinning-process. The same labour-time presents itself in a gold-mass of 15 sh. The price adequate to the value of the 10 lbs. of yarn amounts therefore to 15 sh., the price of one lb. of yarn 1 sh. 6 d.

Our capitalist is taken aback. The *value of the product is equal to the value of the advanced capital*. The advanced *value* has not *valorized (*verwerthet*)* itself, generated no *surplus-value*, *money* has thus *not* transformed itself into *capital*. The price of the 10 lbs. of yarn is 15 sh. and 15 sh. were expended on the commodity-market for the *formation-elements of the product* or, what is the same, the *factors of the labour-process*, 10 sh. for cotton, 2 sh. for the consumed spindle-mass, and 3 sh. for labour-power. The *swollen value of the yarn* helps nothing, for *its* value is only the sum of the values earlier *distributed* over cotton, spindle and labour-power, and out of *such a mere addition of values on hand* a *surplus-value* can now and nevermore spring forth[^13]. These values are now all concentrated onto *one* thing, but so they were in the sum of money of 15 sh. before this splintered itself through three commodity-purchases.

In and for itself this result is not strange. The value of one lb. of yarn is 1 sh. 6 d. and for 10 lbs. of yarn our capitalist would therefore have to pay 15 sh. on the commodity-market. Whether he buys his private house ready-made on the market, or has it built himself, neither of these operations will augment the money laid out in the acquisition of the house.

The capitalist, who knows his way around in vulgar economy, says perhaps that he advanced his money with the *intention* *of making more money out of it*. The road to hell, however, is paved with good intentions and he could just as well have had the intention of making money without producing[^14]. He threatens. One will not catch him again. In future he will buy the commodity ready-made on the market, instead of fabricating it himself. But if all his brother capitalists do the same, where is he to find commodities on the market? And money he cannot eat. He catechizes. One should consider his *abstinence*. He could have squandered his 15 sh. Instead of that he has *productively consumed* them and made yarn out of them. But for that he is after all in possession of yarn instead of pangs of conscience. He must on no account fall back into the role of the *hoarder (*Schatzbildner*)*, who showed us what comes out of asceticism. Besides, where there is nothing, the emperor has lost his right. Whatever the merit of his *renunciation*, there is nothing there to pay for it extra, since the value of the product that comes out of the process is only equal to the sum of the commodity-values thrown into it. Let him calm himself therefore with the thought that virtue is virtue's reward. Instead of that he becomes importunate. The yarn is useless to him. He has produced it for sale. Then let him sell it, or, still simpler, let him in future produce only things for his own requirement, a prescription that his family doctor *Mac Culloch* has already prescribed to him as a tried remedy against the epidemic of overproduction. He rears up defiantly on his hind legs. Was the worker with his own limbs to create labour-constructs (*Arbeitsgebilde*) in the blue air, to produce commodities? Did he not give him the stuff (*Stoff*), *wherewith* and *wherein* alone he can corporealize (*verleiblichen*) his labour? Since now the greatest part of society consists of such have-noughts, has he not rendered to society through his means of production, his cotton and his spindle, an immeasurable *service*, not to the worker himself, whom he on top of that provided with means of life? And is he not to charge for the *service*? Has the worker, however, not rendered him the counter-service of transforming cotton and spindle into yarn? Besides, it is not a matter here of *services*[^15]. A service is nothing than the useful effect (*Wirkung*) of a *use value*, be it of the commodity, be it of the labour[^16]. Here, however, what figures (*gilt*) is *exchange value*. He paid the worker the value of 3 sh. The worker gave him back an exact *equivalent* in the value of 3 sh. set to (*zugesetzt*) the cotton, value for value. Our friend, just now still so capital-arrogant, suddenly assumes the unpretentious posture of his own worker. Has he not himself laboured? not performed the labour of surveillance, of superintendence over the spinner? Does not this his labour also form value? His own overlooker and his manager shrug their shoulders. Meanwhile, however, he has already with a cheerful smile assumed his old physiognomy again. He was fooling us with the whole litany. He does not give a rap for it. He leaves these and similar rotten evasions and hollow humbugs to the professors of political economy specially paid for that. He himself is a practical man, who indeed does not always consider what he says outside of business, but always knows what he does in business.

Let us look closer. The *daily value of labour-power* amounted to 3 sh., because in it itself *half a labour-day* is objectified (*vergegenständlicht*), i.e. because the means of life daily necessary for the production of the labour-power cost half a labour-day. But the past labour that sticks in the labour-power, and the living labour that it can perform, its daily costs of maintenance and its daily expenditure, are two quite different magnitudes. The former determines its exchange value, the other forms its use value. That *half a labour-day* is needed in order to maintain him alive during 24 hours in no way hinders the worker from *labouring (*arbeiten*) a whole day*. The *value* of labour-power and its *valorization (*Verwerthung*)* in the labour-process are thus two different magnitudes. This *value-difference* the capitalist had in view when he bought the labour-power. Its useful property of making yarn or boots was only a conditio sine qua, because labour must be expended in useful form in order to form value. What decided, however, was the *specific use value of this commodity*, to be source of exchange value and of more exchange value than it itself has. This is the specific *service* that the capitalist expects from it. And in doing so he proceeds in accordance with the eternal laws of commodity-exchange. In fact, the seller of labour-power, like the seller of every other commodity, *realizes its exchange value* and *alienates (*veräussert*) its use value*. He cannot obtain the one without giving away the other. The use value of labour-power, *the labour* itself, belongs just as little to its seller as the use value of the sold oil to the oil-merchant. The possessor of money has paid the *daily value* of the labour-power; to him belongs therefore *its use during the day*, the *day-long labour*. The circumstance that the daily maintenance of the labour-power costs only half a labour-day, although the labour-power can act (*wirken*), labour (*arbeiten*) a whole day, that therefore the value which its use creates during a day is twice as great as its own daily value, is a particular piece of luck for the buyer, but thoroughly no wrong against the seller.

Our capitalist has foreseen the casus. The worker therefore finds in the workshop the necessary means of production not only for a six-hour, but for a twelve-hour labour-process. If 10 lbs. of cotton absorbed 6 labour-hours and transformed themselves into 10 lbs. of yarn, then 20 lbs. of cotton will absorb 12 labour-hours and be transformed into 20 lbs. of yarn. Let us consider the *product of the prolonged labour-process*. In the 20 lbs. of yarn 5 labour-days are now objectified (*vergegenständlicht*), 4 in the consumed cotton- and spindle-mass, 1 absorbed by the cotton during the spinning-process. The gold-expression of 5 labour-days, however, is 30 sh. or 1 lb. st. 10 sh. This therefore is the price of the 20 lbs. of yarn. The pound of yarn costs now as before 1 sh. 6 d. But the sum of values of the commodities thrown into the process amounted to 27 sh. The value of the yarn amounts to 30 sh. The *value of the product* has grown by ⅓ above the value advanced for its production. Thus 27 sh. have transformed themselves into 30 sh. They have set (*gesetzt*) a *surplus-value* of 3 sh. The trick has finally succeeded. *Money* is *transformed into capital*.

All conditions of the problem are solved and the laws of commodity-exchange in no way violated. Equivalent was exchanged against equivalent. The capitalist paid as buyer every commodity at its value, cotton, spindle-mass, labour-power. He then did what every other buyer of commodities does. He consumed their use value. The *consumption-process of the labour-power*, which is at the same time *production-process of the commodity*, yielded a product of 20 lbs. of yarn with a value of 30 sh. The capitalist now returns to the market and sells commodity after he has bought commodity. He sells the pound of yarn at 1 sh. 6 d., not a rap *above* or *under* its value. And yet he draws 3 sh. more out of circulation than he originally threw into it. This whole course, the transformation of his money into capital, proceeds *in* the sphere of circulation and proceeds *not* in it. *Through* the mediation of circulation, because conditioned by the *purchase of the labour-power* on the commodity-market. *Not* in circulation, for this only initiates the *valorization-process*, which takes place in the *sphere of production*. And so is "tout pour le mieux dans le meilleur des mondes possibles."

By transforming money into commodities that serve as stuff-formers (*Stoffbildner*) of a new product or as factors of the labour-process, by incorporating (*einverleibt*) living labour-power into their dead objectivity (*Gegenständlichkeit*), the capitalist transforms *value*, past, objectified (*vergegenständlichte*), *dead* labour into *capital, self-valorizing value*, an ensouled monster that begins to "labour" as if it had love in its body.

If we now compare *value-formation-process* and *valorization-process*, the valorization-process is nothing than a value-formation-process *prolonged* beyond a certain point. If the latter lasts only *up to the point* where the value of the labour-power paid by capital is replaced by a new *equivalent*, then it is a simple value-formation-process. If the value-formation-process lasts *beyond this point*, then it becomes a valorization-process.

If we compare further the *value-formation-process* with the *labour-process*, the latter consists in the *actual* labour that produces *use values*. The movement is here considered *qualitatively*, in its particular type and manner, according to purpose and content. *The same labour-process* presents itself in the *value-formation-process* only from its *quantitative* side. It is only still a matter of the *time* which the labour needs for its operation, or of the duration during which the labour-power is expended. Here also the commodities that enter into the labour-process no longer figure (*gelten*) as functionally determined, material (*stoffliche*) factors of the purposively acting (*wirkenden*) labour-power. They *count (*zählen*)* only still as determinate quanta of objectified (*vergegenständlichter*) labour. Whether contained in the means of production or set to (*zugesetzt*) them through the labour-power, the labour *counts* only still according to its measure of time (*Zeitmaß*). It amounts to so many hours, days etc.

It counts, however, only insofar as the time used up for the production of the use value is *socially necessary*. This comprises different things (*Verschiednes*). The labour-power must function under normal conditions. If the spinning-machine is the socially prevailing means of labour for spinning, then a spinning-wheel must not be given into the hand of the worker. Instead of cotton of normal goodness he must not receive trash that tears every moment. In both cases he would use up more than the socially necessary labour-time for the production of a pound of yarn, but this excess time would not form value or money. The *normal* character of the objective (*gegenständlichen*) factors of labour depends, however, not on the worker, but on the capitalist. A further condition is the *normal character* of the *labour-power* itself. In the trade wherein it is applied, it must possess the prevailing average measure (*Durchschnittsmaß*) of skill, dexterity and speed. But our capitalist bought on the labour-market labour-power of normal goodness. This power must be expended in the usual average measure of exertion, with the socially customary *degree of intensity*. Over this the capitalist watches just as anxiously as that no time is wasted without labour. He has bought the labour-power for a determinate period of time. He insists on having his own. He does not want to be *robbed*. Finally --- and for this the same gentleman has an own code pénal --- no counter-purposive consumption of raw material and means of labour may take place, because wasted material or means of labour present superfluously expended quanta of objectified (*vergegenständlichter*) labour, thus do not count and do not enter into the product of value-formation[^17].

One sees: the distinction earlier won out of the analysis of the *commodity* between labour insofar as it creates use value, and *the same* labour insofar as it creates exchange value, has now presented itself as a distinguishing (*Unterscheidung*) of the different sides of the *production-process*.

As *unity of labour-process* and *value-formation-process*, the *production-process* is production-process of commodities; *as unity of labour-process and valorization-process*, it is *capitalist production-process*, capitalist form of commodity-production.

It was earlier remarked that for the valorization-process it is thoroughly indifferent whether the labour appropriated by the capitalist is *simple, social average labour*, or *more complicated labour, labour of higher specific weight*. The labour that figures (*gilt*) as higher, more complicated labour over against social average labour is the *manifestation (*Aeusserung*) of a labour-power* into which higher costs of training (*Bildungskosten*) enter, whose production costs more labour-time and which therefore has a higher exchange value than simple labour-power. If the value of this power is higher, it however also manifests (*äussert*) itself in higher labour and therefore objectifies (*vergegenständlicht*) itself, *in the same spaces of time*, in proportionally *higher values*. Whatever, however, the distinction of degree between spinning-labour and jeweller-labour, the portion of labour whereby the jewel-worker merely replaces the value of his own labour-power distinguishes itself *qualitatively* in no way from the additional portion of labour whereby he creates surplus-value. Now as before the surplus-value comes out only through a *quantitative* excess of labour, through *the prolonged duration of the same labour-process*, in the one case process of yarn-production, in the other case process of jewel-production[^18].

On the other hand, in every value-formation-process the higher labour must always be reduced to social average labour, e.g. one day of higher labour to x days of simple labour[^19]. One saves therefore a superfluous operation and simplifies the analysis through the assumption that the worker applied by capital performs simple social average labour.[^19]:  "Where reference is made to labour as a measure of value, it necessarily implies *labour of one particular kind*. . . . the *proportion* which the other kinds bear to it being easily ascertained." (" *Outlines of Polit. Economy. London* 1832", p. 22, 23.)
