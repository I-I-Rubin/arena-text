### Third Chapter.

## The Production of Absolute Surplus Value.

## 1\) *Labor Process and Valorization Process*

The use of labor-power is *labor itself*. The buyer of labor-power consumes it by having its seller *work*. The latter thereby becomes *actu* self-actuating labor-power (*sich bethätigende*), a *worker*, which he formerly was only *potentia*. In order to present his labor in *commodities*, he must above all present it in *use values*, *things* (*Sachen*) which serve for the meeting of needs of some type or other. It is therefore a particular use value, a determinate *article*, that the capitalist has the worker manufacture. The *production of use values*, or *goods*, does not alter its *universal* nature by the fact that it goes on *for* the capitalist and under his control. The *labor process* is therefore first to be considered in its *abstract* moments, independently of every *determinate social form*.

The labor process is first of all a process between man and nature, a process in which he mediates, regulates and controls his metabolism with nature through his own deed. Man confronts natural matter itself as a natural power. The natural forces belonging to his corporeality, arms and legs, head and hand, he sets in movement, in order to assimilate natural matter in a form usable for his own life. By acting through this movement upon nature outside him and altering it, he at the same time alters his own nature. He develops the potencies slumbering in it and subjects the play of its forces to his own sway. We are not dealing here with the first animal-like, instinctive forms of the labor process. From the condition in which the worker appears on the commodity market as seller of his own labor-power, the condition in which the human labor process had not yet stripped off its first instinct-like form is removed into a primeval background. We suppose the labor process in a form in which it belongs exclusively *to man*. A spider performs operations that resemble those of the weaver, and a bee by the construction of its wax cells puts to shame many a human architect. But what from the outset distinguishes the worst architect from the best bee is that he has built the cell in his head before he builds it in wax. At the end of the labor process a result comes out which at its beginning was already present in the *representation of the worker*, thus already *ideally*. It is not that he only *effects* an alteration of form of the natural; he *actualizes* in the natural at the same time *his purpose*, which he *knows*, which determines the type and manner of his doing as a law, and to which he must subordinate his will. And this subordination is no isolated act. Besides the exertion of the organs that labor, the *will conforming to purpose*, which manifests itself (*äussert*) as *attention*, is required for the whole duration of the labor process, and all the more, the less the labor, through its own content and its type and manner of execution, carries the worker along with it, the less, therefore, he enjoys it as the play of his own bodily and mental forces.

The simple moments of the labor process are *purposive activity* or *labor itself*, its *object* and its *means*.

*The earth* (under which, economically, *water* is also included), as it originally equips man with provisions, ready means of life[^1], is found by him, without his contribution, as the *universal object* of human labor. All things which labor merely detaches from their immediate connection with the earth as a whole are objects of labor found by nature. Thus the fish which, separated from its element of life, water, is caught, the wood which is felled in the primeval forest, the ore which is broken loose from its vein. If, on the other hand, the object of labor is itself already, so to speak, filtered *through prior labor*, we call it *raw material*. For example, the already broken-loose ore which is now washed. All raw material is object of labor, but not every object of labor is raw material. The object of labor is raw material only as soon as it has already undergone an alteration mediated by labor.

The *means of labor* is a thing or a complex of things which the worker pushes between himself and the object of labor and which serve him as the *conductor* of his activity onto this object. He uses the mechanical, physical, chemical properties of things, in order to let them act as means of power upon other things, *in conformity with his purpose*[^2]. The object of which the worker *immediately* takes possession --- apart from the seizing of ready means of life, fruits for example, where his own bodily organs alone serve as means of labor --- is not the object of labor but the means of labor. Thus the natural itself becomes an *organ* of his activity, an organ which he adds to his own bodily organs, prolonging his natural shape, in spite of the Bible. As the earth is his original larder, so it is his original arsenal of means of labor. It supplies him, for example, with the *stone* with which he throws, rubs, presses, cuts, etc. *The earth itself* is a means of labor, but for its service as means of labor in agriculture it in turn presupposes a whole series of other means of labor and an already relatively high development of labor-power[^3]. As soon as the labor process is at all developed to any degree, it already needs means of labor that have been worked upon. In the oldest human caves we find stone tools and stone weapons. Besides worked *stone* and *wood*, the *tamed* animal, thus itself already altered by labor, bred, plays at the beginning of human history the chief role as means of labor[^4].//

The use and the creation of means of labor, although in germ already proper to certain types of animal, characterize the *specifically human labor process*, and *Franklin* therefore defines man as "*a toolmaking animal*", an animal that fabricates tools. The same importance that the structure of bone relics has for the cognition of the organization of extinct animal species, relics of *means of labor* have for the judgement of extinct economic formations of society. It is not *what* is made, but *how*, with what means of labor it is made, that distinguishes economic epochs[^5]. Means of labor are not only the gauge of the development of human labor-power, but also the index of the social relationships under which labor is carried on. Among the means of labor themselves, the *mechanical means of labor*, whose totality one can call the *bone and muscle system of production*, offer much more decisive characteristic marks of a social epoch of production than those means of labor which serve only as containers of the object of labor, and whose totality can quite generally (*allgemein*) be designated the *vascular system of production*, such as pipes, barrels, baskets, jugs, etc. Only in chemical manufacture do they play a significant role.

In a wider sense the labor process counts among its *means*, besides the things which mediate the action of labor upon its object and therefore serve in one way or another as conductors of activity, all *objective conditions* which are required at all in order that the whole process go on. They do not enter directly into the labor process, but without them the labor process cannot go on at all, or only imperfectly. The universal means of labor of this type is again the *earth itself*, for it gives the worker his *locus standi* and his process *the space of action* (*field of employment*). Means of labor of this type already mediated by labor are, for example, workshops, canals, roads, etc.

The *labor process* is thus a process in which the activity of man, through the means of labor, effects an alteration in the object of labor intended from the outset. This process is extinguished in the *product*. Its product is a *use value*, a natural matter assimilated to human needs through alteration of form. Through the process labor has combined with its object. Labor is objectified and the object is worked up. What on the side of the worker appeared in the form of unrest, now appears as a resting property, in the form of being, on the side of the product. He has spun and the product is a spinning.

If one considers the whole process from the standpoint of its result, *the product*, then both, *means of labor* and *object of labor*, appear as *means of production*[^6] and labor itself as *productive labor*[^7].

When a use value comes out of the labor process as *product*, other use values, themselves products of earlier labor processes, enter into the process *as means of production*. The same use value which is the product of one labor process forms the means of production of another. Products are therefore not only result but at the same time *condition* of the labor process.

With the exception of *extractive industry*, which finds its object of labor by nature, such as mining, hunting, fishing, etc. (agriculture only insofar as it in the first instance breaks up virgin earth itself), all branches of industry treat an *object* which is *raw material*, i.e. object of labor already filtered through labor, itself already the product of an earlier labor process. Thus, for example, *seed* in agriculture. Animals and plants, which one is accustomed to consider as products of nature, are not only products, perhaps, of last year's labor, but, in their present forms, products of a transmutation (*Umwandlung*) continued through many generations, under human control, by means of human labor. But as regards the *means of labor* in particular, their enormous majority shows to the most superficial glance the trace of past labor.

Raw material can form the principal substance of a product, or enter into its formation *only* as *auxiliary matter* (*Hilfsstoff*). Auxiliary matter is *consumed by the means of labor*, as coal by a steam engine, oil by the wheel, hay by a draught horse, or *added to the raw material* in order to effect a material alteration in it, as chlorine to unbleached linen, coal to iron, dye to wool, or it supports the *performance of labor* itself, as for example matter used for the lighting and heating of the workplace. The distinction between principal matter and auxiliary matter blurs in chemical manufacture proper, because none of the raw materials employed *appears* again as the substance of the product[^8].

Since every thing possesses many sorts of properties and is therefore capable of different useful applications, the same product can form the raw material of very different labor processes. Corn, for example, is raw material for miller, starch manufacturer, distiller, cattle breeder, etc. It becomes raw material of its own production as seed. Thus coal comes forth as product from the mining industry and enters into it as means of production.

The same product may serve in the same labor process as means of labor and raw material. In the fattening of cattle, for example, where the cattle, the raw material worked upon, are at the same time a means of preparing manure.

A product can exist in a form ready for consumption and nevertheless become anew the raw material of another product, as the grape becomes the raw material of wine. Or the labor process releases its product in forms in which it can be used *only* as raw material of a subsequent labor process. Raw material in this condition is called *semi-manufacture* and would better be called *stage-manufacture*, as for example cotton, thread, yarn, etc. Although itself already product, the original raw material may have to run through a whole graduated series of different labor processes, in which, in a constantly altered shape, it functions constantly anew as raw material, up to the last labor process, which repels it from itself as finished *means of life* or finished *means of labor*.

One sees: whether *a use value* appears as *raw material, means of labor* or *product* depends wholly and entirely on its *determinate function in the labor process*, on the place which it occupies *in it*, and with the change of this place those determinations change.

Through their entry as *means of production* into new labor processes, products therefore lose the character of product. They function only as objective factors of living labor. The spinner relates to the spindle only as the means with which, and to the flax only as the object which, he spins. Certainly one cannot spin without spinning material and spindle. The presence of these products is therefore presupposed at the beginning of the actual spinning process. *In this process itself*, however, it is just as indifferent that flax and spindle are *products of past labor* as it is indifferent in the actual process of nourishment that bread is the *product* of the past labors of farmer, miller, baker, etc. Conversely. If means of production in the labor process assert their character as products of past labor, they do so through their defects. A knife that does not cut, yarn that constantly breaks, etc., remind one vividly of cutler A and yarn-waxer E. In the successful product the mediation of its use-properties through past labor is extinguished.

A machine that does not serve in the labor process is useless. Moreover it falls prey to the destructive power of natural metabolism. Iron rusts, wood rots. Yarn that is not woven or knitted is spoiled cotton. Living labor must seize these things, awaken them from the dead, transform them from merely possible into actual and acting use values. Licked by the fire of labor, appropriated as its bodies, inspirited (*begeistet*) to their functions in the process conforming to their concept and calling, they are indeed also consumed, but purposefully, as formative elements of new use values.

If existing products are not only the *results* but also the *conditions of existence* of the labor process, on the other hand their being thrown into the labor process, thus their contact with living labor, is *the sole means of preserving and actualizing these products of past labor as use values*. The labor process results in *use value* at all only insofar as its product is capable of entering, as *means of life*, into individual consumption or, as *means of production*, into a new labor process.

In the labor process its material elements, its object and its means, are used up *as use values*. Labor devours them. The labor process is thus a *process of consumption*. What distinguishes this *productive consumption* from *individual consumption* is that the latter consumes the products as *means of life of the living individual*, the former as *means of life of labor*, of self-actuating labor-power. The product of individual consumption is therefore *the consumer himself*, the result of productive consumption a *product* distinguished from the consumer.

Insofar as means of labor and object of labor are themselves already *products*, results of past labor processes, the labor process is a process in which *products are consumed in order to create products*, or in which *products serve as means of production of products*. But as the labor process originally goes on only between man and the earth present without his contribution, there still serve in it such means of production as, present by nature, present no combination of natural matter and human labor.

The *labor process*, as we have presented it in its simple and *abstract* moments, is purposive activity for the making of use values, appropriation of the natural for human needs, universal condition of the metabolism between man and nature, eternal natural condition of human life, and therefore independent of every form of this life, rather equally common to all its forms of society. We therefore had no need in our presentation to present the worker in relationship to other workers. Man and his labor on the one side, nature and its matters on the other, sufficed. As little as one tastes from wheat who grew it, so little does one see from this process under what conditions it goes on, whether under the brutal whip of the slave overseer or under the anxious eye of the capitalist, whether Cincinnatus performs it in the tilling of his few jugera, or the savage who fells a beast with a stone[^9].

Let us return to our *capitalist in spe*. We left him after he had bought on the commodity market all the factors necessary for a labor process, the *objective factors* in the *means of production, the subjective factor in labor-power*. The determinate type of these means of production and of this labor-power is governed by the type of labor process for which they are destined (*bestimmt*), whether boots, yarn, etc., are to be made. Our capitalist thus sets about consuming the commodity he has bought, *labor-power*, i.e. *having* the bearer of labor-power, *the worker*, *consume* the means of production through his labor. The *universal* nature of the labor process naturally does not alter by the fact that the worker performs it for the capitalist instead of for himself. But the *determinate type and manner* in which boots are made or yarn is spun cannot at first alter either through the intervention of the capitalist. He must at first take labor-power as he finds it on the market, thus also its labor as it arose in a period when there were as yet no capitalists. The *transformation of the mode of production* itself through the subordination of labor to capital can occur only later and is therefore only later to be considered.

The labor process, as it goes on as the *process of consumption of labor-power by the capitalist*, shows only two peculiar phenomena.

The worker works *under the control of the capitalist*, to whom his labor belongs. The capitalist sees to it that the labor is performed properly and that the means of production are used in conformity with purpose, thus that no raw material is wasted and the instrument of labor is spared, i.e. destroyed only so far as is inseparable from its use in labor.

Secondly, however: *the product is the property of the capitalist*, not of the immediate producer, the worker. The capitalist pays, for example, the *daily value of labor-power*. Its use, like that of every other commodity, for example of a horse that he has hired for a day, thus belongs to him for the day. To the buyer of the commodity belongs the use of the commodity, and the owner of labor-power in fact gives only the use value he has *sold*, by giving *his labor*. From the moment he stepped into the capitalist's workshop, the *use value* of his labor-power, thus its use, *labor*, belonged to the capitalist. The capitalist, through the *purchase of labor-power*, has *incorporated* labor itself, as living ferment, into the dead formative elements of the product, likewise possessed by him. From his standpoint the *labor process* is only the *consumption* of the commodity labor-power he has bought, which, however, he can consume only by *adding means of production* to it. The labor process is a process between things which the capitalist has *bought*, *between things belonging to him*. The *product of this process* therefore belongs to him just as much as the product of the process of fermentation in his wine cellar[^10].

The product --- the property of the capitalist --- is a *use value*, yarn, boots, etc. But although boots, for example, in a certain sense form the basis of social progress, and our capitalist is a decided man of progress, he does not manufacture the boots for their own sake. *Use value* is in general not the thing "*qu'on aime pour lui même*" in *commodity production*. Use values are here produced at all only because and insofar as they are *material* (*materielles*) *substratum, bearers of exchange value*. And for our capitalist two things are at issue. *Firstly* he wants to produce a use value which has an exchange value, an article destined for sale, a *commodity*. And secondly he wants to produce a *commodity* whose value is *higher* than the *sum of values of the commodities required for its production*, the means of production and labor-power, for which he *advanced* his good money on the commodity market. He wants to produce not only a *use value* but a *commodity*, not only use value but exchange value, and not only *value* but also *surplus value*.

In fact, since what is at issue here is *commodity production*, we have so far obviously considered only *one side of the process*. Just as the *commodity* itself is a unity of *use value and exchange value*, its *production process must be a unity of labor process and value-formation process*.

Let us therefore now consider the *production process* also as *value-formation process*.

We know that the value of every commodity is determined by the quantum of *labor materialized* in its use value, by the *labor time socially necessary* for its production. This holds also for the product which resulted for our capitalist from the labor process. What is to be calculated first, then, is *the labor objectified in this product*.

Let it be, for example, *yarn*.

For the making of the yarn, first of all its *raw material* was necessary, for example 10 pounds of cotton. What the value of the cotton is need not first be investigated, for the capitalist bought it on the market at its value, for example at 10 sh. In the *price* of the cotton the labor required for its production is already presented as universal social labor. We will further assume that the *spindle-mass consumed* in working up the cotton, which represents (*repräsentirt*) for us all other means of labor applied, possesses a value of 2 sh. If a mass of gold of 12 sh. is the product of 24 labor hours or two working days, it follows first of all that two working days are objectified in the yarn.

The circumstance that the cotton has altered its form and the consumed spindle-mass has entirely disappeared must not mislead. According to the universal law of value, 10 lbs. of yarn, for example, are an equivalent for 10 lbs. of cotton and ¼ spindle, if the value of 40 lbs. of yarn = the value of 40 lbs. of cotton + the value of a whole spindle, i.e. if *the same labor time* is required to produce both sides of this equality. In this case *the same labor time* presents itself once in the use value yarn, the other time in the use values cotton and spindle. Exchange value is thus indifferent as to whether it appears in yarn, spindle or cotton. That spindle and cotton, instead of lying quietly next to one another, enter in the spinning process into a combination which alters the form of their use value, *transforms them into yarn*, touches their exchange value just as little as if they had been converted, by *simple exchange*, into an equivalent of yarn.

The labor time required for the production of the cotton is part of the labor time required for the production of the yarn, whose raw material it forms, and is therefore contained in the yarn. The same holds for the labor time required for the production of the spindle-mass, without whose wear or consumption the cotton cannot be spun[^11].

As far, then, as the *value of the yarn*, the *labor time* required for its making, comes into consideration, *the different particular labor processes*, *separated* in time and space, which must be run through in order to produce the cotton, to produce the spindle-mass used up, and finally to make yarn from cotton and spindle, can be considered as different successive phases *of one and the same labor process*. All the labor contained in the yarn is *past labor*. That the labor time required for the production of its formative elements had *passed earlier*, stands in the pluperfect, whereas the labor applied immediately to the concluding process, spinning, stands nearer to the present, in the perfect, is a wholly indifferent circumstance. If a determinate mass of labor, for example of 30 working days, is necessary for the building of a house, it alters nothing in the *total quantum* of labor time incorporated in the house that the 30th working day entered into production 29 days later than the first working day. And so the labor time contained in the material of labor and the means of labor can be considered exactly as if it had only been expended at an earlier stage of the spinning process than the quantum of labor expended last in the *form of spinning*.

The values of the means of production, of the cotton and the spindle, expressed in the price of 12 sh., thus form component parts of the *yarn-value*, or of the value of the *product*.

Only two conditions must be fulfilled. First, cotton and spindle must actually have served for the production of a *use value*. In our case, *yarn* must have come out of them.

*Which* use value bears it is indifferent to exchange value, but a *use value* must bear it. Secondly, it is presupposed that only the labor time necessary under the given *social conditions of production* has been used. If, then, only 1 pound of cotton were necessary to spin 1 pound of yarn, only 1 pound of cotton may be consumed in the formation of 1 pound of yarn. The same holds for the spindle. Should the capitalist have the fancy to have spinning done with golden instead of iron spindles, there would nevertheless count in the *yarn-value* only the *socially necessary labor time*, i.e. the labor time necessary for the production of the iron spindle-mass.

We now know the *value-part* which the means of production, cotton and spindle, form in the *yarn-value*. It is equal to 12 sh., or the materiature of two working days. What is at issue now, then, is the value-part which *the labor of the spinner* himself adds to the cotton.

We now have to consider this labor from an entirely other point of view than during the *labor process*. There it was a matter of the purposive activity of transforming cotton into yarn. The more purposive the labor, the better the yarn, all other circumstances presupposed as remaining the same. The labor of the spinner was specifically different from other productive labors, and this difference revealed itself subjectively and objectively (*objektiv*), in the particular purpose of spinning, its particular mode of operation, the particular nature of its means of production, the specific use value of its product. Cotton and spindle serve as *means of life of spinning labor*, but one cannot make rifled cannon with them. Insofar as the labor of the spinner, on the other hand, is *value-forming*, i.e. *source of exchange value*, it is in no way different from the labor of the cannon-borer, or, what lies nearer to us here, from the labors actualized in the means of production of the yarn, the labors of the cotton planter and of the spindle maker. Only because of this *identity* can cotton planting, spindle making and spinning form merely *quantitatively* different parts of *the same* total value, the *yarn-value*. What is at issue here is no longer the *quality*, the constitution and the content of labor, but only its *quantity*. This is simply to be *counted*. We assume that spinning labor is *simple* labor, social average labor. It will be seen later that the contrary assumption would alter nothing in the thing (*Sache*).

During the labor process labor constantly converts itself from the form of unrest into that of being, from the form of movement into that of objectivity. At the end of an hour the spinning movement is presented in a certain quantum of yarn, thus a determinate quantum of labor, *one labor hour*, is *objectified* in the cotton. We say labor hour, for spinning labor figures here only insofar as it is expenditure of labor-power, not insofar as it is the specific labor of *spinning*.

It is now decisively important that during the duration of the process, i.e. of the transformation of cotton into yarn, only the *socially necessary labor time* is consumed. If under *normal*, i.e. average social conditions of production, a pounds of cotton must be transformed into b pounds of yarn during *one* labor hour, then only that working day figures as a working day of 12 hours which transforms 12 × a pounds of cotton into 12 × b pounds of yarn. For only socially necessary labor time counts as *value-forming*.

*Raw material* and *product* appear here in an entirely other light than from the standpoint of the labor process proper. Raw material figures here only as *absorber of a determinate quantum of labor*. Through this absorption it is in fact transformed into yarn, because *spinning labor* was added to it. But the product, the yarn, is now only the gauge of the labor sucked in by the cotton. If in one hour 1⅔ lbs. of cotton are spun, or transformed into 1⅔ lbs. of yarn, then 10 lbs. of yarn indicate 6 labor hours sucked in. Determinate and *empirically established quanta of product* now present nothing but determinate quanta of labor, determinate masses of congealed labor time. They are only *materiature* of one hour, two hours, one day of *social labor*.

That the labor is precisely spinning labor, its material cotton and its product yarn, becomes here just as indifferent as that the *object of labor* is itself already product, thus *raw material*. Were the worker employed in a coal pit instead of in a spinning mill, the object of labor, coal, would be present by nature. Nevertheless a determinate quantum of coal broken loose from the bed, for example one hundredweight, would present a determinate quantum of absorbed labor.

At the *sale of labor-power* it was supposed that its daily value = 3 sh., and that in the latter 6 labor hours are embodied, that this quantum of labor is thus required in order to produce the average sum of the daily means of life of the worker. If our spinner now transforms during one labor hour 1⅔ lbs. of cotton into 1⅔ lbs. of yarn[^12], then in 6 hours 10 lbs. of cotton into 10 lbs. of yarn. During the duration of the spinning process the cotton thus sucks in 6 labor hours. The same labor time presents itself in a quantum of gold of 3 sh. A value of 3 sh. was thus added to the cotton by spinning itself.

Let us now look at the *total value of the product*, the 10 lbs. of yarn. In them 2½ working days are objectified, 2 days contained in cotton and spindle-mass, ½ day of labor sucked in during the spinning process. The same labor time presents itself in a mass of gold of 15 sh. The price adequate to the value of the 10 lbs. of yarn thus amounts to 15 sh., the price of one lb. of yarn 1 sh. 6 d.

Our capitalist is taken aback. The *value of the product is equal to the value of the capital advanced*. The value advanced has not *valorized* itself, has engendered no *surplus value*, *money* has thus *not* transformed itself into *capital*. The price of the 10 lbs. of yarn is 15 sh., and 15 sh. were expended on the commodity market for the *formative elements of the product* or, what is the same, the *factors of the labor process*: 10 sh. for cotton, 2 sh. for the spindle-mass consumed, and 3 sh. for labor-power. The *swollen value of the yarn* is of no help, for *its* value is only the sum of the values formerly *distributed* over cotton, spindle and labor-power, and from *such a mere addition of existing values* no *surplus value* can ever arise[^13]. These values are now all concentrated on *one* thing, but so they were in the sum of money of 15 sh. before this split itself up through three purchases of commodities.

In and for itself this result is not strange. The value of one lb. of yarn is 1 sh. 6 d., and for 10 lbs. of yarn our capitalist would therefore have to pay 15 sh. on the commodity market. Whether he buys his private house ready-made on the market or has it built himself, neither of these operations will increase the money laid out in acquiring the house.

The capitalist, who knows his way about in vulgar economics, perhaps says that he advanced his money with the *intention* of *making more money out of it*. The road to hell, however, is paved with good intentions, and he could just as well have intended to make money without producing[^14]. He threatens. They will not catch him again. In future he will buy the commodity ready-made on the market instead of manufacturing it himself. But if all his brother capitalists do likewise, where is he to find commodities on the market? And money he cannot eat. He catechizes. One should consider his *abstinence*. He could have squandered his 15 sh. Instead he has *consumed* them *productively* and made yarn out of them. But for that he is in possession of yarn instead of pangs of conscience. He must on no account fall back into the role of the *hoarder*, who showed us what comes of asceticism. Besides, where there is nothing, the Emperor has lost his right. Whatever the merit of his *renunciation*, there is nothing there to pay for it extra, since the value of the product that comes out of the process is only equal to the  sum of the commodity values thrown in. Let him then console himself that virtue is virtue's reward. Instead he becomes importunate. The yarn is useless to him. He produced it for sale. Let him sell it, then, or, still more simply, in future produce only things for his own requirements, a recipe which his family physician *Mac Culloch* has already prescribed to him as a proven remedy against the epidemic of overproduction. He rears up defiantly on his hind legs. Was the worker to create labor-formations with his own limbs in the blue air, to produce commodities? Did he not give him the matter *with which* and *in which* alone he can body forth (*verleiblichen*) his labor? Since the greatest part of society consists of such have-nots, has he not rendered society an immeasurable *service* through his means of production, his cotton and his spindle, and not to the worker himself, whom he moreover supplied with means of life into the bargain? And is he not to charge for this *service*? But has the worker not rendered him the counter-service of transforming cotton and spindle into yarn? Besides, what is at issue here is not *services*[^15]. A service is nothing but the useful effect of a *use value*, be it of the commodity, be it of labor[^16]. Here, however, it is *exchange value* that counts. He paid the worker the value of 3 sh. The worker gave him back an exact *equivalent* in the value of 3 sh. added to the cotton, value for value. Our friend, just now so arrogant in his capital, suddenly assumes the unassuming attitude of his own worker. Has he not worked himself? not performed the labor of supervision, of superintendence over the spinner? Does this labor of his not also form value? His own overlooker and his manager shrug their shoulders. Meanwhile, however, with a cheerful smile, he has already resumed his old physiognomy. He was hoaxing us with the whole litany. He does not give a farthing for it. He leaves these and similar lame subterfuges and hollow fiddle-faddle to the professors of political economy, who are specially paid for it. He himself is a practical man, who does not always consider what he says outside of business, but always knows what he does in business.

Let us look more closely. The *daily value of labor-power* amounted to 3 sh., because in it itself *half a working day* is objectified, i.e. because the means of life necessary daily for the production of labor-power cost half a working day. But the past labor which is lodged in labor-power, and the living labor which it can perform, its daily costs of maintenance and its daily expenditure, are two entirely different magnitudes. The former determines its exchange value, the other forms its use value. That *half a working day* is necessary to keep him alive for 24 hours by no means prevents the worker from *working a whole day*. The *value* of labor-power and its *valorization* in the labor process are thus two different magnitudes. This *difference of value* the capitalist had in view when he bought the labor-power. Its useful property of making yarn or boots was only a conditio sine qua, because labor must be expended in a useful form in order to form value. What was decisive, however, was the *specific use value of this commodity*, to be a source of exchange value, and of more exchange value than it itself has. This is the specific *service* which the capitalist expects of it. And in this he proceeds in conformity with the eternal laws of commodity exchange. In fact, the seller of labor-power, like the seller of every other commodity, *realizes its exchange value* and *alienates its use value*. He cannot obtain the one without giving away the other. The use value of labor-power, *labor* itself, belongs just as little to its seller as the use value of oil sold belongs to the oil dealer. The owner of money has paid the *daily value* of labor-power; to him therefore belongs *its use during the day*, the *day-long labor*. The circumstance that the daily maintenance of labor-power costs only half a working day, although labor-power can act, labor, a whole day, that therefore the value which its use creates during one day is twice as great as its own daily value, is a particular piece of good luck for the buyer, but by no means an injustice to the seller.

Our capitalist has foreseen the case. The worker therefore finds in the workshop the means of production necessary not only for a six-hour but for a twelve-hour labor process. If 10 lbs. of cotton sucked in 6 labor hours and transformed themselves into 10 lbs. of yarn, then 20 lbs. of cotton will suck in 12 labor hours and be transformed into 20 lbs. of yarn. Let us consider the *product of the prolonged labor process*. In the 20 lbs. of yarn 5 working days are now objectified, 4 in the cotton- and spindle-mass consumed, 1 sucked in by the cotton during the spinning process. The gold expression of 5 working days, however, is 30 sh. or 1 pound sterling 10 sh. This then is the price of the 20 lbs. of yarn. The pound of yarn costs, as before, 1 sh. 6 d. But the sum of values of the commodities thrown into the process amounted to 27 sh. The value of the yarn amounts to 30 sh. The *value of the product* has grown by ⅑ above the value advanced for its production. Thus 27 sh. have transformed themselves into 30 sh. They have set a *surplus value* of 3 sh. The trick has at last succeeded. *Money* is *transformed into capital*.

All the conditions of the problem are solved and the laws of commodity exchange in no way violated. Equivalent was exchanged for equivalent. The capitalist as buyer paid for each commodity at its value, cotton, spindle-mass, labor-power. He then did what every other buyer of commodities does. He consumed their use value. The *process of consumption of labor-power*, which is at the same time the *production process of the commodity*, yielded a product of 20 lbs. of yarn with a value of 30 sh. The capitalist now returns to the market and sells commodity, after having bought commodity. He sells the pound of yarn at 1 sh. 6 d., not a farthing *above* or *below* its value. And yet he draws 3 sh. more out of circulation than he originally threw into it. This whole course, the transformation of his money into capital, goes on *in* the sphere of circulation and does *not* go on in it. *Through* the mediation of circulation, because conditioned by the *purchase of labor-power* on the commodity market. *Not* in circulation, for this only initiates the *valorization process*, which takes place in the *sphere of production*. And so "tout pour le mieux dans le meilleur des mondes possibles."

By transforming money into commodities which serve as matter-formers of a new product or as factors of the labor process, by incorporating living labor-power into their dead objectivity, the capitalist transforms *value*, past, objectified, *dead* labor, into *capital, value valorizing itself*, an animated monster which begins to "work" as though it had love in its body.

If we now compare *value-formation process* and *valorization process*, the valorization process is nothing but a value-formation process *prolonged* beyond a certain point. If the latter lasts only *up to the point* where the value of labor-power paid by capital is replaced by a new *equivalent*, it is simple value-formation process. If the value-formation process lasts *beyond this point*, it becomes valorization process.

If we further compare the *value-formation process* with the *labor process*, the latter consists in the *actual* labor which produces *use values*. Movement is considered here *qualitatively*, in its particular type and manner, according to purpose and content. *The same labor process* presents itself in the *value-formation process* only from its *quantitative* side. What is at issue is only the *time* which labor needs for its operation, or the duration during which labor-power is expended. Here the commodities which enter into the labor process also no longer figure as functionally determined, material factors of purposively acting labor-power. They *count* only as determinate quanta of objectified labor. Whether contained in the means of production or added by labor-power, labor *counts* only by its time-measure. It amounts to so many hours, days, etc.

It counts, however, only insofar as the time used up for the production of the use value is *socially necessary*. This comprises different things. Labor-power must function under normal conditions. If the spinning machine is the socially dominant means of labor for spinning, the worker must not be put to a spinning wheel. Instead of cotton of normal quality, he must not receive trash that breaks every moment. In both cases he would use up more than the socially necessary labor time for the production of one pound of yarn, but this excess time would not form value or money. The *normal* character of the objective factors of labor, however, depends not on the worker but on the capitalist. A further condition is the *normal character* of *labor-power* itself. In the trade in which it is used, it must possess the dominant average measure of skill, dexterity and quickness. But our capitalist bought on the labor market labor-power of normal quality. This power must be expended in the usual average measure of exertion, with the socially customary *degree of intensity*. Over this the capitalist watches just as anxiously as over no time being wasted without labor. He has bought labor-power for a determinate period of time. He insists on having what is his. He does not want to be *robbed*. Finally --- and for this the same gentleman has a code pénal of his own --- no consumption of raw material and means of labor contrary to purpose may take place, because wasted material or means of labor present superfluously expended quanta of objectified labor, which thus do not count and do not enter into the product of value-formation[^17].

One sees: the distinction obtained earlier from the analysis of the *commodity* between labor insofar as it creates use value and *the same* labor insofar as it creates exchange value has now presented itself as a distinguishing of the different sides of the *production process*.

As *unity of labor process* and *value-formation process*, the *production process* is production process of commodities; *as unity of labor process and valorization process* it is *capitalist production process*, capitalist form of commodity production.

It was remarked earlier that it is wholly indifferent for the valorization process whether the labor appropriated by the capitalist is *simple, social average labor* or *more complicated labor, labor of higher specific gravity*. The labor which figures as higher, more complicated labor over against social average labor is the *manifestation* (*Aeusserung*) *of a labor-power* into which higher costs of formation enter, whose production costs more labor time and which therefore has a higher exchange value than simple labor-power. If the value of this power is higher, it also manifests itself in higher labor and therefore objectifies itself, *in the same periods of time*, in proportionally *higher values*. But whatever the difference of degree between spinning labor and jeweller's labor, the portion of labor by which the jewel-worker only replaces the value of his own labor-power is in no way *qualitatively* distinguished from the additional portion of labor by which he creates surplus value. As before, surplus value comes out only through a *quantitative* excess of labor, through *the prolonged duration of the same labor process*, in the one case the process of yarn production, in the other case the process of jewel production[^18].

On the other hand, in every value-formation process higher labor must always be reduced to social average labor, for example one day of higher labor to x days of simple labor[^19]. One thus spares a superfluous operation and simplifies the analysis by the assumption that the worker employed by capital performs simple social average labor.[^19]:  "Where reference is made to labour as a measure of value, it necessarily implies *labour of one particular kind*. . . . the *proportion* which the other kinds bear to it being easily ascertained." ## Points for you to check

1. **"by ⅑" may be an emendation you don't want.** The German reads *um ⅓ gewachsen*, but 27 → 30 sh. is a growth of ⅑. Later editions read ⅑. If you want the first edition's reading, change it back to "⅓", optionally with "[sic]".
2. **"Was the worker to create labor-formations…" is a choice of mood.** Marx gives the capitalist's indignant questions in reported speech (*Sollte der Arbeiter…*). I kept the subjunctive flavour rather than converting them to direct speech.
3. **"Spinning" for *Gespinnst* may read oddly.** I chose it to mirror *gesponnen/Gespinnst* in "He has spun and the product is a spinning." "Something spun" is the more idiomatic alternative.
4. **"Self-actuating labor-power" may be heavier than you want.** If so, "labor-power actuating itself" stays closer to the reflexive. Your "labor-power in action" is the readable option.
