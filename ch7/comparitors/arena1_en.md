## The Production of Absolute Surplus Value.

## 1\) *Labour-Process and Valorization-Process*

The use of labour-power is *labour itself*. Its purchaser consumes it by making its seller *labour*. Thereby the latter becomes labour-power actuating itself *actu*, *worker*, which earlier he was only *potentia*. In order to present his labour in *commodities*, he must above all present it in *use values*, things(*Sache*) which serve to meet needs of some type. It is therefore a particular use value, a determinate *article*, which the capitalist has the worker manufacture. The *production of use values*, or *goods*, does not alter its *universal* nature because it proceeds *for* the capitalist and under his control. The *labour-process* is therefore first to be considered in its *abstract* moments, independently of every *determinate social form*.

The labour-process is first a process between man and nature, a process wherein he mediates, regulates, and controls his metabolism with nature through his own deed. Man confronts natural material itself as a natural power. The natural forces belonging to his corporeality, arms and legs, head and hand, he sets in movement in order to assimilate natural material to himself in a form usable for his own life. In acting upon nature outside him through this movement and altering it, he at the same time alters his own nature. He develops the potencies slumbering in it and subjects the play of its forces to his own obedience. We are not concerned here with the first animal-like, instinctive forms of the labour-process. Into the prehistoric background has receded, behind the condition wherein the worker appears on the commodity-market as seller of his own labour-power, the condition wherein the human labour-process had not yet cast off its first instinct-like form. We presuppose the labour-process in a form wherein it belongs exclusively to *man*. A spider performs operations resembling those of the weaver, and a bee puts many a human architect to shame by the construction of its wax cells. But what distinguishes the worst architect from the best bee from the outset is that he has built the cell in his head before he builds it in wax. At the end of the labour-process there emerges a result which, at its beginning, was already present in the *representation of the worker*, hence already *ideally*. Not that he merely *effects* a form-change of the natural: in the natural he at the same time *actualizes his purpose*, which he *knows*, which determines the type and manner of his doing as law, and to which he must subordinate his will. And this subordination is no isolated act. Apart from the exertion of the organs which labour, the *purposive will*, manifesting itself as *attention*, is required for the whole duration of the labour-process, and the more so the less the worker is carried away by labour through its own content and manner of execution, the less, therefore, he enjoys it as the play of his own bodily and mental powers.

The simple moments of the labour-process are *purposive activity* or *labour itself*, its *object* and its *means*.

*The earth* (under which, economically, *water* is also included), as it originally equips man with provisions, ready means of life[^1], is found, without his intervention, as the *universal object* of human labour. All things which labour merely detaches from their immediate connection with the earth as a whole are objects of labour found ready in nature. Thus the fish which, separated from its life-element, water, is caught; the wood felled in the virgin forest; the ore broken loose from its vein. If, on the other hand, the object of labour is itself already, so to say, filtered *through previous labour*, we call it *raw material*. For example, ore already broken loose, which is now washed. All raw material is an object of labour, but not every object of labour is raw material. The object of labour is raw material only once it has already undergone an alteration mediated through labour.

The *means of labour* is a thing or a complex of things which the worker interposes between himself and the object of labour, and which serve him as *conductors* of his activity upon this object. He uses the mechanical, physical, and chemical properties of things in order to make them act, in accordance with his purpose, as means of power upon other things[^2]. The object of which the worker takes possession *immediately*—apart from the seizure of ready means of life, fruits for example, in which his own bodily organs alone serve as means of labour—is not the object of labour, but the means of labour. Thus the natural itself becomes an *organ* of his activity, an organ which he adds to his own bodily organs, thereby extending his natural shape, despite the Bible. As the earth is his original storehouse of provisions, so is it his original arsenal of means of labour. It supplies him, for example, with the *stone* with which he throws, rubs, presses, cuts, and so on. *The earth itself* is a means of labour, but its service as a means of labour in agriculture in turn presupposes a whole series of other means of labour and a relatively high development of labour-power[^3]. As soon as the labour-process is at all developed, it already requires worked means of labour. In the oldest human caves we find stone tools and stone weapons. Alongside worked *stone* and *wood*, at the beginning of human history the *tamed*, that is, itself already altered by labour, bred *animal* plays the principal role as a means of labour[^4].// 

The use and creation of means of labour, although germinally peculiar to certain animal species, characterize the *specifically human labour-process*, and *Franklin* therefore defines man as “*a toolmaking animal*,” an animal manufacturing tools. The same importance which the construction of bone relics has for knowledge of the organization of vanished animal genera, relics of *means of labour* have for the assessment of vanished economic formations of society. Not *what* is made, but *how*, with what means of labour it is made, distinguishes the economic epochs[^5]. Means of labour are not only the measure of the development of human labour-power, but also the index of the social relations under which labour is performed. Among the means of labour themselves, the *mechanical means of labour*, the totality of which may be called the *bone-and-muscle system of production*, offer far more decisive characteristic marks of a social epoch of production than do those means of labour which serve only as containers for the object of labour, and whose totality may quite generally be designated the *vessel-system of production*, such as pipes, casks, baskets, pots, and so forth. Only in chemical manufacture do they play a significant role.

In the wider sense, the labour-process counts among its *means*, apart from the things which mediate the effect of labour upon its object and thus serve in one way or another as conductors of activity, all *objective conditions* which are in general required for the entire process to proceed. They do not enter directly into the labour-process, but the labour-process cannot proceed at all without them, or only imperfectly. The universal means of labour of this type is again *the earth itself*, for it gives the worker the *locus standi* and gives his process *its field of action* (*field of employment*). Means of labour of this type already mediated through labour are, for example, work-buildings, canals, roads, and so forth.

The *labour-process* is therefore a process wherein the activity of man, through the means of labour, effects in the object of labour an alteration intended from the outset. This process is extinguished in the *product*. Its product is a *use value*, natural material assimilated to human needs through a form-change. Through the process labour has combined with its object. Labour is objectified, and the object is worked over. What appeared on the side of the worker in the form of unrest, now appears, on the side of the product, as a resting property, in the form of being. He has spun, and the product is spun yarn.

If one considers the entire process from the standpoint of its result, *the product*, then both, *means of labour* and *object of labour*, appear as *means of production*[^6], and labour itself as *productive labour*[^7].

When a use value comes out of the labour-process as a *product*, other use values, themselves products of earlier labour-processes, enter the process *as means of production*. The same use value which is the product of one labour-process constitutes the means of production of another. Products are therefore not only result, but at the same time *condition* of the labour-process.

With the exception of *extractive industry*, which finds its object of labour ready in nature, such as mining, hunting, fishing, and so forth, (agriculture only insofar as it first breaks up virgin earth itself), all branches of industry process an *object* which is *raw material*, that is, an object of labour already filtered through labour, itself already the product of an earlier labour-process. Such is, for example, *seed* in agriculture. Animals and plants, which one is accustomed to regard as natural products, are not only perhaps products of the labour of the preceding year but, in their present forms, products of a transformation continued through many generations, under human control, by means of human labour. As regards the *means of labour* in particular, however, their enormous majority shows even to the most superficial glance the trace of past labour.

Raw material can form the principal substance of a product, or enter into its formation *only* as an *auxiliary material*. The auxiliary material is *consumed by the means of labour*, as coal by a steam-engine, oil by a wheel, hay by a draught-horse; or it is *added to the raw material* in order to effect a material alteration in it, such as chlorine to unbleached linen, coal to iron, dye to wool; or it supports the *performance of labour* itself, as, for example, materials used for lighting and heating the place of labour. The distinction between principal material and auxiliary material vanishes in manufacture properly chemical, because none of the raw materials employed *appears again* as the substance of the product[^8].

Since every thing possesses manifold properties and is therefore capable of different useful applications, the same product can form the raw material of very different labour-processes. Grain, for example, is raw material for the miller, starch-manufacturer, distiller, cattle-breeder, and so on. As seed, it becomes raw material of its own production. Thus coal emerges from mining industry as product and enters it as a means of production.

The same product may serve in the same labour-process as means of labour and raw material. In cattle-fattening, for example, the cattle, the worked raw material, are at the same time means of manure-production.

A product can exist in a form ready for consumption and nevertheless become anew the raw material of another product, as the grape becomes the raw material of wine. Or the labour-process discharges its product in forms wherein it can be used *only* as raw material for a following labour-process. Raw material in this condition is called *semi-finished product*, and would better be called *stage-product*, such as cotton, thread, yarn, and so forth. Although itself already a product, the original raw material may have to pass through a whole succession of different labour-processes, wherein, in ever-changing shape, it functions anew and anew as raw material up to the final labour-process, which casts it off as a finished *means of life* or a finished *means of labour*.

One sees: whether *a use value* appears as *raw material, means of labour*, or *product*, depends wholly and entirely upon its *determinate function in the labour-process*, upon the place which it occupies *in it*, and with the change of this place those determinations change.

By entering new labour-processes as *means of production*, products therefore lose the character of product. They function only as objective factors of living labour. The spinner relates to the spindle only as the means wherewith, and to flax only as the object which he spins. Certainly, one cannot spin without spinning material and spindle. The presence of these products is therefore presupposed at the beginning of the actual spinning-process. *In this process itself*, however, it is just as indifferent that flax and spindle are *products of past labour* as, in the actual nourishment-process, it is indifferent that bread is the *product* of the past labours of farmer, miller, baker, and so forth. Conversely, if means of production assert their character as products of past labour in the labour-process, they do so through their defects. A knife that does not cut, yarn that continually breaks, and so forth, forcibly remind one of knife-maker A and yarn-dresser E. In the successful product the mediation of its use-properties through past labour is extinguished.

A machine which does not serve in the labour-process is useless. Moreover, it succumbs to the destructive force of natural metabolism. Iron rusts, wood rots. Yarn which is not woven or knitted is spoiled cotton. Living labour must seize these things, awaken them from the dead, transform them from merely possible into actual and acting use values. Licked by the fire of labour, appropriated as its bodies, inspired in the process to their functions appropriate to their concept and calling, they are indeed also consumed, but purposively, as formative elements of new use values.

If existing products are not only the *results*, but also the *conditions of existence* of the labour-process, their being thrown into the labour-process, hence their contact with living labour, is, on the other hand, *the only means of preserving and actualizing these products of past labour as use values*. The labour-process results in *use value* at all only insofar as its product is capable of entering, as a *means of life*, individual consumption, or, as a *means of production*, a new labour-process.

In the labour-process its material elements, its object and its means, are consumed *as use values*. Labour devours them. The labour-process is therefore a *consumption-process*. This *productive consumption* differs from *individual consumption* in that the latter consumes the products as *means of life of the living individual*, the former as *means of life of labour*, of labour-power actuating itself. The product of individual consumption is therefore *the consumer himself*, the result of productive consumption a *product* distinct from the consumer.

Insofar as means of labour and object of labour are themselves *products*, results of past labour-processes, the labour-process is a process wherein *products are consumed in order to create products*, or wherein *products serve as means of production of products*. But as the labour-process originally proceeds only between man and the earth present without his intervention, means of production which are present by nature and represent no combination of natural material and human labour still serve in it.

The *labour-process*, as we have presented it in its simple and *abstract* moments, is purposive activity for the production of use values, appropriation of the natural for human needs, universal condition of the metabolism between man and nature, eternal natural condition of human life, and therefore independent of every form of this life, rather equally common to all its forms of society. We therefore did not need, in our presentation, to present the worker in relation to other workers. Man and his labour on the one side, nature and its materials on the other, sufficed. Just as little as one can taste from wheat who has grown it, just as little can one see from this process under what conditions it proceeds, whether under the brutal whip of the slave-overseer or under the anxious eye of the capitalist, whether Cincinnatus performs it in cultivating his few jugera, or the savage who kills a beast with a stone[^9].

Let us return to our *capitalist in spe*. We left him after he had bought on the commodity-market all factors necessary for a labour-process, the *objective factors* in the *means of production*, the subjective factor in labour-power. The determinate type of these means of production and of this labour-power follows from the type of labour-process for which they are intended, whether boots, yarn, and so forth, are to be made. Our capitalist therefore sets about consuming the commodity bought by him, *labour-power*, that is, making the bearer of labour-power, *the worker*, consume the means of production through his labour. The *universal* nature of the labour-process naturally does not alter because the worker performs it for the capitalist instead of for himself. But even the *determinate manner and way* in which boots are made or yarn spun cannot at first alter through the intervention of the capitalist. He must first take labour-power as he finds it on the market, and therefore also its labour as it arose in a period when there were as yet no capitalists. The *transformation of the mode of production* itself through the subsumption of labour under capital can occur only later, and is therefore to be considered only later.

The labour-process, as it proceeds as *consumption-process of labour-power by the capitalist*, shows only two characteristic phenomena.

The worker labours *under the control of the capitalist*, to whom his labour belongs. The capitalist takes care that labour is properly performed and that the means of production are employed purposively, so that no raw material is wasted and the instrument of labour is spared, that is, destroyed only insofar as this is inseparable from its use in labour.

Secondly, however: *the product is the property of the capitalist*, not of the immediate producer, the worker. The capitalist pays, for example, the *daily value of labour-power*. Its use, like that of every other commodity, for example of a horse which he has hired for a day, therefore belongs to him for the day. The use of the commodity belongs to the purchaser of the commodity, and the possessor of labour-power in fact gives only the use value *sold* by him when he gives *his labour*. From the moment when he entered the capitalist's workshop, the *use value* of his labour-power, hence its use, *labour*, belonged to the capitalist. Through the *purchase of labour-power*, the capitalist has *incorporated* labour itself, as living ferment, into the dead formative elements of the product, which likewise belong to him. From his standpoint, the *labour-process* is only the *consumption* of the commodity labour-power bought by him, which he can, however, consume only by adding means of production to it. The labour-process is a process between things which the capitalist has *bought*, *between things belonging to him*. The *product of this process* therefore belongs to him quite as much as the product of the fermentation-process in his wine-cellar[^10].

The product—the property of the capitalist—is a *use value*, yarn, boots, and so forth. But although boots, for example, in a certain respect form the basis of social progress, and our capitalist is a decided man of progress, he does not manufacture boots for their own sake. The *use value* is in general not the thing “*qu'on aime pour lui même*” in *commodity-production*. Use values are here produced at all only because and insofar as they are *material substrate, bearer of exchange value*. And our capitalist is concerned with two things. *First*, he wants to produce a use value which has exchange value, an article intended for sale, a *commodity*. And secondly, he wants to produce a *commodity* whose value is *higher* than the *sum of values of the commodities required for its production*, of the means of production and labour-power, for which he *advanced* his good money on the commodity-market. He wants to produce not only a *use value*, but a *commodity*, not only use value, but exchange value, and not only *value*, but also *surplus value*.

In fact, since this is a matter of *commodity-production*, we have hitherto evidently considered only *one side of the process*. As the *commodity* itself is unity of *use value and exchange value*, its *production-process* must be unity of labour-process and value-formation process.

Let us therefore now consider the *production-process* also as a *value-formation process*.

We know that the value of every commodity is determined by the quantum of labour *materialized* in its use value, by the *socially necessary labour-time* for its production. This also holds for the product which emerged for our capitalist as the result of the labour-process. What must first be calculated, therefore, is *the labour objectified in this product*.

Let it be, for example, *yarn*.

For the production of yarn, its *raw material* was first necessary, for example 10 pounds of cotton. What the value of the cotton is need not first be investigated, for the capitalist bought it on the market at its value, for example at 10 sh. In the *price* of cotton the labour required for its production is already presented as universal social labour. Let us further assume that the spindle-mass *consumed in the processing of the cotton*, which represents for us all other means of labour laid out, possesses a value of 2 sh. If a gold-mass of 12 sh. is the product of 24 labour-hours or two labour-days, it follows first that two labour-days are objectified in the yarn.

The circumstance that the cotton has altered its form and that the consumed spindle-mass has wholly disappeared must not mislead us. According to the universal law of value, for example, 10 lbs. of yarn are an equivalent for 10 lbs. of cotton and ¼ of a spindle, if the value of 40 lbs. of yarn = the value of 40 lbs. of cotton + the value of one whole spindle, that is, if *the same labour-time* is required to produce both sides of this equality. In this case *the same labour-time* presents itself on one occasion in the use value yarn, on the other occasion in the use values cotton and spindle. Exchange value is therefore indifferent to whether it appears in yarn, spindle, or cotton. That spindle and cotton, instead of lying quietly beside one another, enter in the spinning-process into a combination which alters the form of their use value, *transforms them into yarn*, affects their exchange value just as little as if they had been converted by *simple exchange* into an equivalent of yarn.

The labour-time required for the production of cotton is part of the labour-time required for the production of yarn, of which it forms the raw material, and is therefore contained in the yarn. The same holds for the labour-time required for the production of the spindle-mass, without the wear or consumption of which the cotton cannot be spun[^11].

Insofar, then, as the *value of yarn*, the *labour-time* required for its production, is concerned, the *different particular* labour-processes, separated in time and space, which must be passed through in order to produce the cotton, to produce the spindle-mass used up, and finally to make yarn from cotton and spindle, can be considered as different successive phases *of one and the same labour-process*. All labour contained in the yarn is *past labour*. That the labour-time required for the production of its formative elements had *passed earlier*, stands in the pluperfect, whereas the labour immediately expended for the final process, spinning, is closer to the present, stands in the perfect, is an entirely indifferent circumstance. If a determinate mass of labour, for example of 30 labour-days, is necessary for the construction of a house, it alters nothing in the *total quantum* of labour-time incorporated in the house that the thirtieth labour-day entered production 29 days later than the first labour-day. And thus the labour-time contained in the material of labour and the means of labour can quite well be considered as though it had been expended only at an earlier stage of the spinning-process than the quantum of labour expended last in the *form of spinning*.

The values of the means of production, cotton and spindle, expressed in the price of 12 sh., therefore form constituent parts of the *value of yarn*, or of the value of the *product*.

Only two conditions must be fulfilled. First, cotton and spindle must really have served for the production of a *use value*. In our case, *yarn* must have become of them.

*Which* use value bears it is indifferent to exchange value, but a *use value* must bear it. Secondly, it is presupposed that only the labour-time necessary under the given *social conditions of production* has been employed. If, therefore, only 1 pound of cotton is necessary in order to spin 1 pound of yarn, only 1 pound of cotton may have been consumed in the formation of 1 pound of yarn. The same holds for the spindle. If the capitalist had the fancy to have spinning done with golden instead of iron spindles, only the *socially necessary labour-time*, that is, the labour-time necessary for the production of the iron spindle-mass, would nevertheless figure in the *value of yarn*.

We now know the *value-part* which the means of production, cotton and spindle, form in the *value of yarn*. It is equal to 12 sh., or to the materiature of two labour-days. It is now a matter of the value-part which *the labour of the spinner* himself adds to the cotton.

We must now consider this labour from a wholly different standpoint from that occupied during the *labour-process*. There the matter was purposive activity, transforming cotton into yarn. The more purposive the labour, the better the yarn, all other circumstances presupposed as remaining equal. The labour of the spinner was specifically different from other productive labours, and this difference manifested itself subjectively and objectively, in the particular purpose of spinning, its particular manner of operation, the particular nature of its means of production, the specific use value of its product. Cotton and spindle serve as *means of life of spinning labour*, but drawn cannon cannot be made with them. Insofar, on the other hand, as the labour of the spinner is *value-forming*, that is, *source of exchange value*, it is in no way different from the labour of the cannon-borer, or, what is closer to us here, from the labours realized in the means of production of yarn, the labours of the cotton-planter and spindle-maker. Only because of this *identity* can cotton-growing, spindle-making, and spinning form merely quantitatively different parts of the *same* total value, the *value of yarn*. Here it is no longer a matter of the *quality*, constitution, and content of labour, but only of its *quantity*. This is simply to be *counted*. We assume that spinning labour is *simple* labour, social average labour. One will later see that the opposite assumption would alter nothing in the matter.

During the labour-process labour continually passes from the form of unrest into that of being, from the form of movement into that of objectivity. At the end of an hour, the spinning movement is presented in a determinate quantum of yarn, hence a determinate quantum of labour, *one labour-hour*, is *objectified* in the cotton. We say labour-hour, for spinning labour figures here only insofar as it is expenditure of labour-power, not insofar as it is the specific labour of *spinning*.

It is now decisively important that during the duration of the process, that is, the transformation of cotton into yarn, only *socially necessary labour-time* be consumed. If, under *normal*, that is, average social conditions of production, a pounds of cotton must be transformed into b pounds of yarn during *one* labour-hour, only that labour-day figures as a labour-day of 12 hours which transforms 12 × a pounds of cotton into 12 × b pounds of yarn. For only socially necessary labour-time counts as *value-forming*.

*Raw material* and *product* appear here in a wholly different light than from the standpoint of the labour-process proper. Raw material figures here only as *absorber of a determinate quantum of labour*. Through this absorption it is indeed transformed into yarn, because *spinning labour* is added to it. But the product, yarn, is now only the measure of the labour absorbed by the cotton. If 1⅔ lbs. of cotton are spun in one hour, or transformed into 1⅔ lbs. of yarn, then 10 lbs. of yarn indicate 6 absorbed labour-hours. Determinate and *empirically established quanta of product* now present nothing but determinate quanta of labour, determinate masses of congealed labour-time. They are only the materiature of one hour, two hours, one day of *social labour*.

That the labour is precisely spinning labour, its material cotton, and its product yarn, is here just as indifferent as that the *object of labour* itself is already a product, hence *raw material*. If, instead of in the spinning-mill, the worker were employed in the coal-mine, the object of labour, coal, would be present by nature. Nevertheless, a determinate quantum of coal broken loose from the seam, for example a hundredweight, would present a determinate quantum of absorbed labour.

In the *sale of labour-power* it was presupposed that its daily value = 3 sh., and that 6 labour-hours are embodied in the latter, that this quantum of labour is therefore required in order to produce the average sum of the worker's daily means of life. If our spinner now transforms 1⅔ lbs. of cotton into 1⅔ lbs. of yarn during one labour-hour[^12], he transforms 10 lbs. of cotton into 10 lbs. of yarn in 6 hours. During the duration of the spinning-process, cotton therefore absorbs 6 labour-hours. The same labour-time presents itself in a gold-quantum of 3 sh. Cotton has therefore had a value of 3 sh. added to it by spinning itself.

Let us look at the *total value of the product*, the 10 lbs. of yarn. In them 2½ labour-days are objectified, 2 days contained in cotton and spindle-mass, ½ day of labour absorbed during the spinning-process. The same labour-time presents itself in a gold-mass of 15 sh. The price adequate to the value of 10 lbs. of yarn therefore amounts to 15 sh., the price of one lb. of yarn to 1 sh. 6 d.

Our capitalist is taken aback. The *value of the product is equal to the value of the advanced capital*. The advanced *value* has not *valorized itself*, has generated no *surplus value*, *money* has therefore *not* transformed itself into *capital*. The price of 10 lbs. of yarn is 15 sh., and 15 sh. were expended on the commodity-market for the *formative elements of the product*, or, what is the same, the *factors of the labour-process*, 10 sh. for cotton, 2 sh. for the consumed spindle-mass, and 3 sh. for labour-power. The *swollen value of the yarn* is of no help, for *its* value is only the sum of the values previously *distributed* over cotton, spindle, and labour-power, and from such a bare addition of existing values a *surplus value* can never and ever arise[^13]. These values are now all concentrated in *one* thing, but so they were in the money-sum of 15 sh. before this was fragmented through three commodity-purchases.

This result is not in and for itself strange. The value of one lb. of yarn is 1 sh. 6 d., and for 10 lbs. of yarn our capitalist would therefore have to pay 15 sh. on the commodity-market. Whether he buys his private house ready-made on the market or has it built himself, neither of these operations will increase the money laid out in acquiring the house.

The capitalist, versed in vulgar economics, perhaps says that he advanced his money with the *intention* of making *more money out of it*. The road to hell, however, is paved with good intentions, and he might just as well have had the intention of making money without producing[^14]. He threatens. He will not let himself be caught again. In future he will buy the commodity ready-made on the market, instead of manufacturing it himself. But if all his brother capitalists do likewise, where is he to find commodity on the market? And he cannot eat money. He catechizes us. One should consider his *abstinence*. He could have squandered his 15 sh. Instead, he has *consumed them productively* and made yarn out of them. But for that very reason he possesses yarn instead of pangs of conscience. He must by no means fall back into the role of the *treasure-former*, who showed us what comes of asceticism. Besides, where there is nothing, the Emperor has lost his right. Whatever the merit of his *renunciation*, there is nothing there to pay him extra, since the value of the product which comes out of the process is only equal to the sum of the commodity-values thrown into it. Let him therefore console himself that virtue is virtue's reward. Instead, he becomes importunate. The yarn is useless to him. He produced it for sale. Let him therefore sell it, or, still more simply, let him henceforth produce only things for his own requirements, a recipe which his house-doctor *Mac Culloch* has already prescribed to him as a proved means against the epidemic of over-production. He rises stubbornly on his hind legs. Should the worker, with his own limbs, create formations of labour in the blue air, produce commodities? Did he not give him the material *with which* and *in which* alone he can embody his labour? Since the greatest part of society consists of such have-nots, has he not rendered society an immeasurable *service* through his means of production, his cotton and his spindle, and has he not rendered it to the worker himself, whom he furthermore supplied with means of life? And should he not charge for the *service*? But has the worker not rendered him the counter-service of transforming cotton and spindle into yarn? Besides, the matter here is not one of *services*[^15]. A service is nothing but the useful effect of a *use value*, whether of commodity or of labour[^16]. But here the issue is *exchange value*. He paid the worker the value of 3 sh. The worker gave him back an exact *equivalent* in the value of 3 sh. added to the cotton, value for value. Our friend, just now so capital-proud, suddenly assumes the unassuming bearing of his own worker. Has he not himself laboured? Has he not performed the labour of supervision, of overseeing the spinner? Does not this labour of his also form value? His own overlooker and manager shrug their shoulders. Meanwhile, however, with a cheerful smile he has already resumed his old physiognomy. He was fooling us with the whole litany. He does not give a farthing for it. He leaves these and similar idle evasions and hollow fancies to the professors of political economy paid specially for that purpose. He himself is a practical man, who, though not always considering what he says outside business, always knows what he does in business.

Let us look more closely. The *daily value of labour-power* amounted to 3 sh. because in it itself *half a labour-day* is objectified, that is, because the means of life daily necessary for the production of labour-power cost half a labour-day. But the past labour contained in labour-power, and the living labour which it can perform, its daily costs of maintenance and its daily expenditure, are two wholly different magnitudes. The former determines its exchange value; the latter forms its use value. That *half a labour-day* is necessary in order to keep it alive for 24 hours does not in the least prevent the worker from *working a whole day*. The *value* of labour-power and its *valorization* in the labour-process are therefore two different magnitudes. The capitalist had this *difference of value* in view when he bought labour-power. Its useful property of making yarn or boots was only a conditio sine qua, because labour must be expended in useful form in order to form value. But decisive was the *specific use value of this commodity*, to be source of exchange value and of more exchange value than it itself has. This is the specific *service* which the capitalist expects from it. And in doing so he proceeds in accordance with the eternal laws of commodity-exchange. In fact, the seller of labour-power, like the seller of every other commodity, *realizes its exchange value* and *alienates its use value*. He cannot retain the one without giving away the other. The use value of labour-power, *labour* itself, belongs just as little to its seller as the use value of oil sold belongs to the oil-dealer. The possessor of money has paid the *daily value* of labour-power; its use during the day, *the labour of the whole day*, therefore belongs to him. The circumstance that the daily maintenance of labour-power costs only half a labour-day, although labour-power can act, work, for a whole day, and that consequently the value which its use creates during a day is twice as large as its own daily value, is a special fortune for the purchaser, but in no way an injustice to the seller.

Our capitalist foresaw the case. The worker therefore finds in the workshop the necessary means of production not only for a six-hour, but for a twelve-hour labour-process. If 10 lbs. of cotton absorbed 6 labour-hours and transformed themselves into 10 lbs. of yarn, then 20 lbs. of cotton will absorb 12 labour-hours and be transformed into 20 lbs. of yarn. Let us consider the *product of the prolonged labour-process*. In the 20 lbs. of yarn, 5 labour-days are now objectified, 4 in the consumed cotton- and spindle-mass, 1 absorbed by cotton during the spinning-process. The gold-expression of 5 labour-days is, however, 30 sh., or £1 10 sh. This, therefore, is the price of the 20 lbs. of yarn. The pound of yarn still costs 1 sh. 6 d. But the sum of values of the commodities thrown into the process amounted to 27 sh. The value of the yarn amounts to 30 sh. The *value of the product* has grown by ⅓ above the value advanced for its production. Thus 27 sh. have transformed themselves into 30 sh. They have set a *surplus value* of 3 sh. The trick has at last succeeded. *Money* has transformed itself into *capital*.

All conditions of the problem are solved and the laws of commodity-exchange in no way violated. Equivalent was exchanged for equivalent. As purchaser, the capitalist paid every commodity at its value, cotton, spindle-mass, labour-power. He then did what every other purchaser of commodities does. He consumed their use value. The *consumption-process of labour-power*, which is at the same time *production-process of commodity*, yielded a product of 20 lbs. of yarn with a value of 30 sh. The capitalist now returns to the market and sells commodity after buying commodity. He sells the pound of yarn at 1 sh. 6 d., not a farthing *over* or *under* its value. And yet he draws 3 sh. more from circulation than he originally threw into it. This entire course, the transformation of his money into capital, occurs *in* the sphere of circulation and does *not* occur in it. *Through* the mediation of circulation, because conditioned by the *purchase of labour-power* on the commodity-market. *Not* in circulation, because this only introduces the *valorization-process*, which occurs in the *sphere of production*. And thus “tout pour le mieux dans le meilleur des mondes possibles.”

In transforming money into commodities which serve as material constituents of a new product or as factors of the labour-process, in incorporating living labour-power into their dead objectivity, the capitalist transforms *value*, past, objectified, *dead* labour into *capital, value valorizing itself*, an animated monster which begins to “labour” as though it had love in its body.

If we now compare *value-formation process* and *valorization-process*, the valorization-process is nothing but a value-formation process *prolonged* beyond a certain point. If the latter lasts only *up to the point* at which the value of labour-power paid by capital is replaced by a new *equivalent*, then it is a simple value-formation process. If the value-formation process lasts *beyond this point*, it becomes valorization-process.

If we further compare the *value-formation process* with the *labour-process*, the latter consists in *actual* labour which produces *use values*. Movement is considered here *qualitatively*, in its particular type and manner, according to purpose and content. The *same labour-process* presents itself in the *value-formation process* only from its *quantitative* side. The matter is now only the *time* which labour needs for its operation, or the duration during which labour-power is expended. Here, too, the commodities entering the labour-process no longer figure as functionally determinate, material factors of purposively acting labour-power. They figure only as determinate quanta of objectified labour. Whether contained in the means of production or added by labour-power, labour figures only according to its measure of time. It amounts to so many hours, days, and so forth.

It figures, however, only insofar as the time consumed in the production of the use value is *socially necessary*. This comprises different matters. Labour-power must function under normal conditions. If the spinning-machine is the socially dominant means of labour for spinning, the worker must not be given a spinning-wheel in his hand. Instead of cotton of normal quality, he must not receive rubbish which tears at every moment. In both cases he would consume more than the socially necessary labour-time for the production of a pound of yarn, but this surplus time would not form value or money. The *normal* character of the objective factors of labour, however, depends not upon the worker but upon the capitalist. A further condition is the *normal character* of *labour-power* itself. In the occupation wherein it is employed, it must possess the prevailing average measure of skill, dexterity, and rapidity. But our capitalist bought labour-power of normal quality on the labour-market. This power must be expended in the customary average measure of exertion, with the socially usual *degree of intensity*. The capitalist watches over this just as anxiously as he watches that no time is wasted without labour. He has bought labour-power for a determinate period of time. He insists on having what is his. He does not wish to be *robbed*. Finally—and for this the same gentleman has a code pénal of his own—there must take place no consumption of raw material and means of labour contrary to purpose, because squandered material or means of labour represent quanta of objectified labour expended superfluously, and therefore do not figure and do not enter into the product of value-formation[^17].

One sees: the distinction earlier gained from the analysis of the *commodity* between labour insofar as it is use value and *the same* labour insofar as it creates exchange value, has now presented itself as a distinction between the different sides of the *production-process*.

As *unity of labour-process* and *value-formation process*, the *production-process* is production-process of commodities; as *unity of labour-process and valorization-process*, it is *capitalist production-process*, capitalist form of commodity-production.

It was noted earlier that for the valorization-process it is wholly indifferent whether the labour appropriated by the capitalist is *simple, social average labour*, or *more complex labour, labour of higher specific gravity*. Labour which figures as higher, more complex labour in comparison with social average labour is the *expression of a labour-power* into which higher costs of education enter, whose production costs more labour-time, and which therefore has a higher exchange value than simple labour-power. If the value of this power is higher, it manifests itself in higher labour and therefore objectifies itself, *in the same periods of time*, in proportionately *higher values*. But whatever the difference of degree between spinning-labour and jeweller's labour, the portion of labour whereby the jeweller merely replaces the value of his own labour-power differs *qualitatively* in no way from the additional portion of labour whereby he creates surplus value. As before, surplus value emerges only through a *quantitative* excess of labour, through *the prolonged duration of the same labour-process*, in the one case process of yarn-production, in the other case process of jewellery-production[^18].

On the other hand, in every value-formation process higher labour must always be reduced to social average labour, for example one day of higher labour to x days of simple labour[^19]. One therefore saves a superfluous operation and simplifies analysis by assuming that the worker employed by capital performs simple social average labour.[^19]: “Where reference is made to labour as a measure of value, it necessarily implies *labour of one particular kind*. . . . the *proportion* which the other kinds bear to it being easily ascertained.” (“*Outlines of Polit.
